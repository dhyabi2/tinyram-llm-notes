## P-0103  SSD Offloading for LLM Mixture-of-Experts Weights Considered Harmful in Energy Efficiency
- Authors/venue: Kwanhee Kyung, Sungmin Yun, Jung Ho Ahn (Seoul National Univ.); arXiv 2508.06978v1 [cs.AR], 9 Aug 2025 (IEEE Computer Architecture Letters style, 4 pages). Only v1 read.
- Setup (Sec. IV, Table I): NOT a measurement. An analytical/simulation tool (modified from the Duplex simulator, github scale-snu/SSD-offloading) of a DGX-H100-like node: 8 H100 80GB, NVLink4 450 GB/s per direction, per GPU 256 GB DDR5-7200 and one NVMe SSD, GPUDirect Storage, aggregate CPU-mem and SSD read bandwidth set equal to the NVLink bandwidth. BF16. Models: Mixtral 8x7B (47B, 1 node, 32 layers, 8 experts top-2), DeepSeek-R1 (671B, 4 nodes, 57 layers, 256 routed top-8 + 1 shared), Llama 4 Maverick (400B, 128 experts top-1), Llama 3.3 70B dense. Token-to-expert assignment is a synthetic UNIFORM random distribution; L_in = 1024, first decode step only. Energy constants: Flash read 102.4 pJ/b, HBM 4.2 pJ/b, DDR5 9.2 pJ/b (Fig. 2).
- Numbers:
  - Energy per token SSD vs HBM baseline: 3.8x to 12.5x (Mixtral), 4.7x to 9.8x (R1) depending on batch (Sec. V-A, Fig. 4). SSD vs CPU-memory offload: 2.1x to 3.6x. Fig. 1 (R1, batch 1024, 1024 input tokens): SSD 4.9x HBM, CPU memory 3.1x lower than SSD... (text: "4.9x vs HBM, 3.1x more than offloading to CPU memory").
  - SSD access = 80% of per-token energy for R1 at batch 1024 (73% Mixtral).
  - Amortisation saturates "around batch size 16 for Mixtral and 64 for DeepSeek-R1" (Sec. V-A).
  - Latency: with prefetch + NVLink5, SSD offload latency penalty down to 1.32x (Mixtral) and 1.25x (R1) (Fig. 5).
  - Llama 4 on SSD beats Llama 3.3 on HBM in energy only if Flash read energy falls ~10x (to ~10 pJ/b) and only at batch < 3 (Fig. 6).
  - My arithmetic: with uniform routing unique experts = N(1-(1-k/N)^B): Mixtral B=16 -> 7.9 of 8; R1 B=64 -> 223 of 256 (87%). So the "saturation" points are a property of the uniform-random formula the simulator assumes, not a measured routing result. For our 512 top-10 model B=64 -> 366 of 512 (matches C.md).
- Catalog numbers: "saturates ... about batch 16 Mixtral, 64 DeepSeek-R1" CORRECT. "flash reads still over..." (text cut off in the CSV; the paper says the SSD energy remains dominant, 73-80% at batch 1024). First-pass JSON: all its numbers CORRECT (3.8-12.5x, 4.7-9.8x, 2.1-3.6x, saturation points). First pass OMITTED that the study is simulator-only with uniform-random routing (not measured hardware, no real traces) - this is a correction of emphasis.
- Conditions / transfer: energy, not speed; GPU+HBM datacentre; batch 1..1024. transfers = partly: only the formula-level saturation behaviour carries over (it is the same math as our random-routing column). The energy conclusion is irrelevant to a speed question and is on GPUs. At batch 1 the paper itself says sparsity is maximal (its optimistic case).
- Quality loss: none (lossless offload). Ablation: none (batch sweep and Flash-energy sweep only). Limitations stated: current-technology SSD energy; future Flash could change it; no validation against hardware stated.
- Contradicts our findings? No. It is consistent with C.md (unique/layer reaches 366/512 only at K=64).

## P-0106  ExpertFlow: Optimized Expert Activation and Token Allocation for Efficient Mixture-of-Experts Inference (v1) / ExpertFlow: Efficient Mixture-of-Experts Inference via Predictive Expert Caching and Token Scheduling (v2)
- Authors: v1 (23 Oct 2024) Xin He, Shunkang Zhang, Yuxin Wang, Haiyan Yin, Zihao Zeng, Shaohuai Shi, Zhenheng Tang, Xiaowen Chu, Ivor Tsang, Yew Soon Ong (A*STAR, HKUST, HKBU, NTU, HIT-Shenzhen, HKUST-GZ); v2 (2 Apr 2026, DAC '26) adds Kaijie Tang and re-runs. Both read; catalog URL is v1, the first pass used v2 numbers.
- Setup: single NVIDIA A40 48GB + Xeon Gold 6338; experts offloaded to CPU RAM. Models: Switch-32/64/128 (top-1, 12 layers, 2.0/3.8/7.4B params), Mixtral-8x7B (top-2); v2 adds Qwen1.5-MoE (60 experts, top-4) and DeepSeek-MoE (64, top-6). Tasks WMT16, XSUM (v2 adds Alpaca, AIME2024). Baselines: Cache-MoE (per-layer LRU cache), SE-MoE (layer preload with ring schedule), Pregated-MoE; Mixtral/Qwen/DeepSeek compared only against Cache-MoE. Batch size 4-32 (Switch), 4-16 (others). "Cache size" = experts kept per layer (v2) / proportion (v1). Batch 1 is not evaluated. ## P-0107  Dynamic Expert Sharing: Decoupling Memory from Parallelism in Mixture-of-Experts Diffusion LLMs
- Authors: Hao (Mark) Chen, Zhiwen Mo, Royson Lee, Qianzhou Wang, Da Li, Shell Xu Hu, Wayne Luk, Timothy Hospedales, Hongxiang Fan (Imperial College London, Samsung AI Cambridge). arXiv 2602.00879v1 [cs.LG], 31 Jan 2026; preprint dated 3 Feb 2026.
- Setup: models LLaDA2.0-Mini 16B (256 experts per layer, top-8 as read from Fig. 6 label "experts=256" and Sec. text "top-8") and LLaDA-MoE-7B-A1B (64 experts); block-wise parallel decoding with block length 32 (16 prefix + 16 suffix cache tokens), Fast-dLLM KV cache, confidence threshold 0.9. Hardware: NVIDIA B200 with CUDA 13.1, Xeon 6960P. Benchmarks HumanEval, MBPP, GSM8K, MATH500. Baselines (reimplemented for dLLMs): top-k=4, NAEE, MC-MoE.
- Numbers:
  - Table 1 (LLaDA2.0-Mini): Vanilla avg unique experts T = 84 per layer (0.98 GB per layer), DES-Vote beta=0.15 T=38 (0.45 GB) with 99.5% avg relative accuracy; DES-Seq k=3 T=45, 97.2%; DES-Vote beta=0.10 T=25, 96.4%; top-k=4: T=56, 74.8%; NAEE 46.6%; MC-MoE 46.7%. LLaDA-MoE-7B: Vanilla T=59; DES-Vote T=38, 97.6%; DES-Seq k=3 T=42, 96.9%.
  - Fig. 5: MoE-layer latency -38.0% (LLaDA2.0-Mini) and -31.9% (LLaDA-MoE); end-to-end GPU kernel time -8.2% to -14.3% (which model has which not recoverable from text extraction).
  - Fig. 1a: unique activated experts vs degree of parallelism (4..128): "grows nearly linearly" (abstract wording). Sec 3 states the uniform-routing formula N(1-(1-K/N)^N), the same as our random-routing column.
  - My arithmetic: block 32 on 256 experts top-8: random = 256(1-(1-8/256)^32) = 163 unique, observed vanilla 84, so measured sharing 8x32/84 = 3.05x vs random 1.57x (ratio of real to random 1.94). Our C.md same-domain K=32 on 128 experts: 4.59 vs random 2.29 (ratio 2.0). The two independent measurements agree that within-context tokens share about 2x more than random.

- Limitations: not stated in a dedicated section that I found; lossy; requires diffusion LLMs.
- Contradicts us? No.

- Authors: Zhiyuan Fang, Yuegui Huang, Zicong Hong, Yufeng Lyu, Wuhui Chen, Yue Yu, Fan Yu, Zibin Zheng (Sun Yat-sen Univ., HKUST, Huawei, Peng Cheng Lab). arXiv 2502.06888v1 [cs.LG], 9 Feb 2025 (ASPLOS-style template).

  - Mixtral 8x22B on one 3090: 1.325 tok/s (conclusion "over 1.3"); on H800 with memory use cut 74.5% about 40 tok/s.

Only paper in this batch with a real SSD (1 GB/s).
- Quality loss: none for the main method; Klotski(q) quantised variant adds 1-3% throughput (22.604 vs 22.414), accuracy not reported in what I read. - Contradicts us? No. The ablation shows that all of the gain comes from running many batches to share each loaded layer, which is the "B tokens together" idea; C.md measured that with a shared cache the same idea gives 0.75-1.0x at B=2..8 because union working sets exceed the cache.

# Per-family sections

## Family screen 1 #16 (K independent prompts in lock-step; P-0103..P-0107) and #17 (B tokens together; P-0109, P-0110)
All seven papers are throughput/energy papers with the weights in fast-ish memory (host RAM, HBM) and large batches, thousands of tokens per expert, or a simulator. None runs CPU-only inference, none streams a 100B+ model from NVMe at batch 1, none uses a 512-expert top-10 model. Only Klotski has a real SSD (1 GB/s, bf16 8x22B), and its throughput is aggregate.

Further prior art found (read at the depth stated):
- Lynx: Enabling Efficient MoE Inference through Dynamic Batch-Aware Expert Selection, Gupta et al. (Georgia Tech), arXiv 2411.08982 (v3, 18 May 2026 read in full text for abstract/intro/offloading section). Reduces the number of distinct experts per decode batch by remapping low-confidence token-expert assignments; up to 1.30x lower latency, within 1% accuracy (v3; earlier version reported 1.55x per the search listing, not checked); with Mixtral-8x7B offloaded (19 of 94 GB to CPU) on one A100, boosts offloading by 18-31% (Fig. 15); notes offloaded inference is ~50x slower than on-device. What is new in OUR variant: none of the read papers combines (a) K or B independent prompts served in lock-step by a single user machine with (b) a small NVMe-backed cache (2-8 token footprints) and (c) a CPU-bound decode. Closest: Klotski (multi-batch layer sharing with real SSD, GPU, aggregate throughput) and Lynx/XShare (batch-level expert set reduction, but lossy and GPU). The idea itself (share each loaded expert among co-scheduled requests) is well known. What our work adds is a measurement in the new regime (per-user collapse, cache loss), which the papers do not contain.

Statements that change or support what we believe about our own measurements:
1. Supports: DES paper gives an independent real-routing data point (256 experts, block 32, tokens in one context): 3.05x sharing vs 1.57x random (ratio 1.94), matching our same-domain/random ratio of 2.0 at K=32 on 128 experts. Our optimistic "same-domain" series is therefore in line with the real within-context number; the figure for independent prompts is not measured by any of these papers.
2. Supports: all headline speedups (10.3x, 85x, 10x, 31x) collapse to 1.1-3x or less against the strongest baseline in the same paper (MoE-Lightning 1.16-2.1x vs FlexGen; Klotski 2.23x vs FlexGen, +15% at batch 128; ExpertFlow 1.94-2.12x vs LRU cache; MoE-Gen 3.6-5.3x vs MoE-Lightning*).
3. Nothing found contradicts C.md. One caution: P-0103's saturation (batch 16/64) is a derived property of its uniform-random assumption, not a measured result, so it cannot be used as independent confirmation of our 125B saturation (K=64, 366/512), only of the same formula.
