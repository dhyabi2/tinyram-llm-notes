## Changes to what we should believe about OUR measurements
1. Cache hit rate at 16 GB is probably lower than we modelled. Public benchmarks on the 125B-class model itself (llama.cpp PR threads, single-user, unverified) give hit 0.72 at 13% of experts, 0.77 at 16%, 0.89 at 28%, and an offline per-layer LRU study 62/76/88/91/97% at 7.8/14/25/31/62.5% of experts; our 30B-footprint mapping gave 0.95 at the 16 GB point (about 14.7% of experts). Throughput consequence: 16 GB base path 4.19 -> about 3.18 tok/s (see COMPARISON.md). Action: re-run the 8/16/32 GB sweeps on routing from the real 512-expert model.
2. Top-k quality gate is not established for the target. Our +45.6% perplexity at top-4 is OLMoE (no router renormalisation, no shared expert). A study on Qwen3.5-397B (512 experts, top-10) finds 10 -> 5 costs 2.10 MMLU / 4.20 GSM8K with standard renormalisation but 0.55 / 0.80 when the renormalisation denominator stays anchored to the native top-10 mass; flash-style K=4 claims are unsupported; Qwen3-30B plain cut to k0=3 loses ~30 AIME points (Opportunistic Expert Activation Tables 1,2,7,9). Action: test denominator anchoring (free at inference); a plain-renormalisation Qwen3-30B perplexity run is in progress.
3. Replay order does not inflate our simulators: independent LRU implementations agree exactly (21/21 cases); randomising order within a layer changes hit rate by <=0.03%, reversing layer order by <=0.3%. (A cited paper warns recency-policy simulations can be inflated 27-29% by inconsistent replay; ours are not.)
4. Prefill coverage: real prefill traces on OLMoE (64 experts, top-8) touch 52% of experts at 13 tokens, 57% at 30, 81% at 51, 91% at 104, 93% at 250; on Qwen3-30B (128 experts) 57% at 84 tokens and 68% at 228 tokens (decode-trace proxy gave 55-66% at ~200). Papers report 80-98% on 32-64-expert models; 512 experts unmeasured.
5. Smarter eviction has a bounded upside: learned policies beat LRU by +7% (Qwen3-30B) to +22% (OLMoE) decode speed; Belady-LRU hit gap 0.13-0.22 resembles ours; SpecMD/FlashMoE argue LRU fits expert access poorly.
6. Cache-aware routing: the published training-free variants (Cache-Conditional Experts 2412.00099, a 512-expert gate-weight-guarded variant) substitute far fewer picks than our 24% swap (7.5% of calls at a 25% GPU cache, GSM8K -1.8 points); a quality-free 50% swap is unsupported (inference-time biasing raises perplexity 6.35 -> 10.60 in one paper).
7. Forced-token (jump-forward) share: 12.7% of tokens on a public benchmark vs our synthetic 44-61%; ideal bound ~1.15x, not 1.1-2.3x. Naive forced insertion breaks canonical tokenisation (97% non-canonical without token healing).
8. Dispatch overhead is 1-7% of decode: independent data (ik_llama.cpp fused MoE +3-4% prompt / +1-2% generation on CPU; ASAP +6%).
9. Stock-mmap gap may be larger than our emulation: published stock-style mmap gaps 2.6-6.9x (Flash-MoE ~5x, SGLang pack 3.3-6.9x, SSD-LLaMA 2.6-4.1x), all GPU/phone/Apple; our emulation gave 1.5-2.4x. Action: run real stock llama.cpp mmap with restricted RAM (Mac ballast measurement 0.33 tok/s at ~8x is the first such point).
10. Unmodelled cache term: a fixed expert cache can lose when RAM is scarce because it competes with dense weights and the OS page cache (BigMoeOnEdge, Flash-MoE: 4.80 vs 5.29 tok/s).
11. Confirmations from outside: previous-token persistence 0.42-0.46 (Budgeting Bytes 46.7% on Qwen3-30B); lossless prefetch 1.00-1.075x (HOBBIT 1.01-1.05x, router lookahead 0.3%); co-activation reorder ~+5% over prefetch (mbolt 1.053x vs ours 1.045x); union bytes under speculation (AcceptMoE: 2.70-2.83x expert bytes vs plain decoding); LRU cliff below one footprint (BigMoeOnEdge, Routide); flat expert weight spectrum (MoBE, CARDAN); lookup kernels lose to int8-dot on AVX2 (Intel paper, CARDAN, FASQ).
12. Our negative cross-layer-residual result should be worded "no byte-level cross-layer residual" (our probe: gate matrices only, no neuron alignment); functional redundancy between layers is published (CS-MoE, Meta Expert); within-layer learned basis works on Qwen3-30B (SLBF -24% params, 84.3 vs 84.9 accuracy).

## Novelty (what is actually ours)
Mechanisms are known in every family. Not found in any paper: lossless accounting of expert bytes vs an LRU cache of 2-4 footprints on NVMe -> CPU at batch 1 for a 512-expert top-10 model; passive remote expert store at batch 1; incremental prefill of the typed prompt for a streamed-expert MoE; compute-bound speculation on CPU with measured union cost. Public prior art already streams a 125B-class 512-expert model from flash (Flash-MoE 397B 4.36 tok/s on a 48 GB Mac with pread; BigMoeOnEdge; a Reddit 125B-class engine 20 tok/s on RTX 3060+16 GB RAM, unverified), so "stream a 125B from disk on a small machine" is not novel.
