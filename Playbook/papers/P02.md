## Published-method rows

### P-0032: DraftExpert: Expansion-Aware Self-Speculative Decoding for End-Device MoE Inference (Dengke Han, arXiv 2607.24434 v1, 27 Jul 2026, preprint, 9 pp)
- url opened: yes (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.24434 (abstract read via /abs and full text via /html))
- setting: DeepSeek-V2-Lite and Moonlight-16B-A3B (27 layers, 64 routed + 2 shared, top-6). Two platforms: RTX 4090 24 GB with experts in host RAM (BF16, PCIe), and Snapdragon 8 Elite phone (Hexagon NPU, llama.cpp Q4_0, experts staged from flash). Baseline: autoregressive expert-offloaded decoding on the same platform, plus best training-free shared+top-r drafter.
- headline: Table 2: speedup vs AR 1.36x (DS, GPU, 2.19->2.99 tok/s), 1.52x (DS, phone, 10.18->15.47), 1.29x (ML, GPU, 1.94->2.50), 1.61x (ML, phone, 8.50->13.69); average 1.45x; acceptance 84-87%, prefetch hit 86-88% (abstract). Training-free top-r drafter is slower than AR in all four cells.
- catalog summary: **accurate**
- transfers to our setting: No. Needs a trained per-layer draft expert (training cost not reported), models are 16B with 64 experts, GPU or NPU compute, and the AR baseline has no stated LRU size. The phone flash path (10 tok/s for a 16B Q4_0 model) is nowhere near disk-bound like a 69 GB file on 8-32 GB RAM.
- strongest caveat: Only 2 small models and 2 setups; baseline cache policy unspecified; about 12-14% of prefetched experts are wasted; gain is against AR, not against AR plus a tuned cache.
- notes: The page itself says exact verification still exposes expert misses, i.e. the union cost is real; consistent with our byte finding but the paper avoids it by a fixed-footprint draft path (shared + top-1 + trained draft expert), which is a different idea from 'k positions expert-major'.

### P-0033: AcceptMoE: Commitment-Weighted Self-Sizing Verifier Expert Sets for Efficient MoE Speculative Decoding (Liang, Chen, Mo, Wang, Li, Ma, Luk; Imperial College London / Tile-AI; arXiv 2608.02989 v1, 4 Aug 2026)
- url opened: yes (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.02989)
- setting: Qwen3-Instruct, Qwen3-Coder (30B-A3B class) and GPT-OSS-120B; EAGLE-3 draft tree (64 draft tokens), SGLang, batch 1, greedy, 256 tokens, 20 prompts, median of 3. Resident: RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell. Offload: RTX 5090 32 GB, 48 expert slots per layer on GPU, experts in pinned host RAM (BF16; GPT-OSS native MXFP4). Baselines: Vanilla AR, Standard SD (EAGLE-3 with native routing), MoE-Spec-style fixed budgets.
- headline: Mean 1.290x over Standard SD with all experts resident (range 1.217-1.339x over 12 model-task pairs); 2.06x over Standard SD under physical offloading; H2D traffic per output token -73.6% to -77.1% and cache hit +10.8 to +14.2 points vs Standard SD (Table 2); mean accuracy 0.27 points below Standard SD; residency-aware pruning alone cuts traffic 38.6-48.6% and adds 4.6-15.1% throughput.
- catalog summary: **accurate**
- transfers to our setting: Partly, as evidence for the direction only. It confirms that Standard SD moves MORE expert bytes than vanilla AR under offloading and has a lower hit rate, which matches our simulation (k>1 does not beat an LRU path). But AcceptMoE's win comes from restricting which experts the target may use (a lossy change to the model's routing), on PCIe host RAM, with a 32 GB GPU holding 48 slots/layer.
- strongest caveat: 2.06x is relative to Standard SD, not to AR with a good cache; the method changes the output distribution (accuracy -0.27 pts mean, per-pair -1.83 to +1.22); GPU-resident dense parts, draft model and KV on GPU.
- notes: Text says Standard SD is faster than Vanilla AR on all four GPT-OSS pairs under offload, so SD can still beat AR there; the paper gives no k=1-with-large-LRU baseline in our sense. A 125B model (GPT-OSS-120B) is among the targets but with GPU-resident slots.

### P-0034: MoE-Spec: Expert Budgeting for Efficient Speculative Decoding (McDanel, Li, Surineni, Khaitan; arXiv 2602.16052 v1, 17 Feb 2026, cs.LG)
- url opened: yes (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.16052)
- setting: OLMoE-1B-7B (64 experts, k=8), Qwen3-30B-A3B (128, k=8), Mixtral-8x7B (8, k=2); EAGLE-3 (EAGLE-1 for Mixtral), tree of 63 tokens; NVIDIA A100 80 GB (Mixtral on 2 GPUs); 80 samples per benchmark, 5 benchmarks; experts fully resident in HBM (no offloading experiments).
- headline: Speedup over EAGLE in 27 of 30 model-benchmark configs; average relative gain 27% (Mixtral), 16% (Qwen3-30B), 6% (OLMoE); example Mixtral MBPP T=0 2.3x vs EAGLE 1.7x; OLMoE: a 127-token tree activates 54 of 64 experts per layer (Fig. 2b); at tree 255 EAGLE-3 loads 54 experts for 1.85x, MoE-Spec loads 32 for 2.1x (Fig. 4). Abstract: 10-30% over EAGLE-3.
- catalog summary: **accurate**
- transfers to our setting: No for the speedup (HBM-bound A100, nothing offloaded, comparison is to EAGLE-3). Yes for the premise: the union of experts of a draft tree approaches the whole layer at tree sizes 30-100, which is the same mechanism as our union-fraction measure.

## Memory check
Core idea (verify several positions together so each expert is read once, and the union of experts across draft tokens is the cost) is well known: Cascade (2025), MoESD (2025), MoE-Spec/SP-MoE/SpecMoEOff/EVICT/AcceptMoE/EcoSpec (2025-26). Closest published work: AcceptMoE and SpecMoEOff for offloading, Cascade for the 'speculation can hurt MoE' result. Our variant differs in regime: batch 1, CPU compute, a 2-4 footprint LRU in front of NVMe. I know of no paper that compares expert-major k-grouping against a small LRU on the same trace; they compare to AR or to Standard SD.

## Verdict #4
- Novelty: known (the mechanism), variant only in the regime (disk-bound CPU, tiny LRU).
- Published evidence for a speedup in our regime: no. Every positive number is GPU (A100, RTX 3090/4090/5090, 4xGPU), PCIe host RAM, comparison to AR or Standard SD, often large cache fractions (48 slots of 128 experts per layer in AcceptMoE) or lossy routing restriction (AcceptMoE, MoE-Spec).
- Our measurement: k=8 vs k=1 at 2 footprints 1.01x; k=4 1.35x worse; 4 footprints flat (0.98x); only vs a cacheless baseline 2.19x. The published AcceptMoE data point 'Standard SD transfers more expert bytes than AR and has a lower hit rate' agrees with this.
- Inapplicable headline numbers: DraftExpert 1.45x (trained draft expert, 16B models, GPU/NPU, baseline AR with unspecified cache); AcceptMoE 2.06x (vs Standard SD, lossy, GPU); MoE-Spec 10-30% (resident A100, vs EAGLE-3); the Reddit 'dead' claim is one anecdote.

## Memory check
Lossless small-draft + big-model verify is textbook. The MoE-specific twist (verification cost = union of experts; adaptive K) is known from Cascade (MICRO 2025) and later. The confidence gate that lets the draft emit tokens without big-model verification is the cascade / deferral literature (Faster Cascades, Big Little Decoder), applied to dense models; no MoE+offloading version found. Our differences: draft resident on a CPU laptop, big model on NVMe, and the gate decides whether to touch the disk at all.

## Verdict #5
- Novelty: known for lossless draft+union verify and for adaptive K; open/variant for 'confidence gate decides whether to read experts from disk' in a lossy cascade, but only the gate half and it is lossy.
- Published evidence for a speedup in our regime: unclear. Cascade shows speculation can be up to 1.5x SLOWER on MoE (in-GPU); SP-MoE 1.35x avg is vs other GPU offloaders; MoE-Spec / EVICT / hackmd are in-GPU; no one measures a disk-bound CPU setup. Draft quality and acceptance on a 125B MoE are unknown.
- Our measurement: bytes gain per accepted token at a=0.7 at most 1.00x with an LRU in front; k=2 tok/s gain 1.31-1.60x only if batch-2 compute runs at 311 GFLOP/s (unmeasured); screen's 17x and 11 tok/s refuted by arithmetic.
- Inapplicable headline numbers: SP-MoE 1.07-3.5x (GPU, PCIe, DRAM tier); Cascade (+57% Mixtral code, FP8 on a 48 GB GPU); Faster Cascades 1.85-2.5x (T5 dense, lossy); hackmd -3 to -45% / +27.5% (model fully on GPU).
- Tests suggested by the papers: AcceptMoE's own control shows 'Standard SD vs AR' under a real expert cache is the number to measure on the target stack; a batch-2..4 matmul GFLOP/s measurement on the target CPU decides the k=2 case.

## Memory check
Idea (make routing temporally consistent so a small cache works) is well known in both forms: inference-time cache-prior (Skliar et al., 2024, TMLR 2025) and fine-tuned/trained (ReMoE 2026, Sticky Routing 2026, Joint Router Adaptation 2026). Closest work: ReMoE (SSD-backed llama.cpp). Our variant differs in regime only: a 125B model with 512 experts and a cache of 2-8 footprints; nobody has published results at that scale.

## Verdict #6
- Novelty: known.
- Published evidence for a speedup in our regime: yes in direction, unclear in size. ReMoE on Jetson/llama.cpp/NVMe: 1.77-1.99x decode with a small cache (6 experts/layer) and +15.7% relative hit rate, but on a 15.7B model, with an mmap baseline that decodes under 2 tok/s, so part of the gain is probably page-cache effects; its vLLM PCIe test shows only +8.4%. Cache-Conditional Experts: >2x vs best LRU on phones, lossy, 14B-class models.
- Our measurement: f=0.5 stickiness gives hit 0.763 vs 0.549 at 2 footprints = 1.33x serial / 1.12x overlap, matching the screen; prefill claim (7x TTFT, 12.5% of experts touched) refuted (128-token window union still 0.49).
- Hard blocker for us: a 125B router fine-tune (ReMoE took about 7.9 h on one 80 GB GPU for 15.7B) is out of reach on our hardware; only the training-free cache-prior variant is testable locally, and it is lossy.
- Inapplicable numbers: ExpertFlow (A40 GPU, Switch/learned predictor), vLLM issue (GPU cache RFC), Not-All-Models (oracle cache hit rate, no throughput), ReMoE Jetson 44-50% (small model, mmap baseline).

## Memory check
Using 2-3 bit experts for offloaded MoE is the oldest idea in this batch: Eliseev and Mazur (Dec 2023), EdgeMoE (2023), MoQE (2023), HOBBIT (2024). The 'smaller bytes per token means faster when I/O-bound' mechanism is folklore and is confirmed by the Reddit anecdote in reverse (a smaller quant did not help when streamed bytes per token did not shrink). Our regime differs (125B, 512 experts, 8-32 GB RAM, bytes cache sized in footprints) but no paper isolates the equal-RAM comparison we simulate.

- Novelty: known.
- Published evidence for a speedup in our regime: unclear. # Cross-cutting findings and cautions
- Positive results in this literature are overwhelmingly GPU + host-DRAM-over-PCIe, with caches covering a third to two thirds of experts per layer, and baselines that are AR, Standard SD or naive/mmap offloading; none uses a tuned LRU of 2-4 footprints in front of an NVMe on a CPU.
- Several headline numbers are lossy (AcceptMoE, MoE-Spec, Cache-Conditional Experts, ReMoE, HOBBIT miss replacement, 2-bit experts) and should not be compared to lossless baselines.
- The strongest corroboration of our measurements: AcceptMoE Table 2 (Standard SD moves more expert bytes than AR and lowers hit rate), Cascade (MoE speculation up to 1.5x slower), BigMoeOnEdge limitations (streaming incompatible with speculation), Reddit anecdote (bytes per token govern speed; CPU compute caps at about 2.8 tok/s).
- The strongest threat to novelty: ReMoE and Cache-Conditional Experts (#6), Eliseev/HOBBIT/EdgeMoE (#7), the whole 2025-26 spec-MoE cluster (#4/#5).
- Reading limits: for HTML pages, WebFetch returned a model-written summary of the page, not raw text; for PDFs I read extracted text (AcceptMoE, MoE-Spec, SP-MoE, ReMoE, Eliseev, HOBBIT, Specter, EVICT) but not every section (MoE-Spec, SP-MoE and EVICT by targeted reading of setup, results and limitations). Papers dated after my training data (all 2026 items) are known only from what was read here. Venue claims in the catalog for P-0040 (ICLR 2025), P-0045 (ICLR 2026) and P-0043 (ICML 2026, only a search listing) were not confirmed on the paper pages.
