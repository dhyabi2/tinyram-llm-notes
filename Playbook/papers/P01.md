# Papers check, batch P01 (screen 1 families #0, #1, #2, #3)

Read-only check done 2026-10-29 by fetching each catalog URL (WebFetch page text, no downloads). All 26 catalog rows were opened; where the given URL was a binary PDF the fetch tool could not decode (EStream, DAOP, Fiddler, FlashMoE, NAEE), the arXiv html or abs version of the same paper was read instead (said per row). No row is "unverified", but several pages gave only partial detail (noted). Local measurements quoted are from Playbook/pubtech/A.md.

Counts: 26 rows, 26 opened; summary accuracy: 13 accurate, 12 partly, 1 wrong (P-0025). Duplicates: P-0002 and P-0011 are the same paper (MoE-Spec); P-0005 and P-0014 are the same paper (SpecMoEOff); P-0013 is a bundle of four papers.

## Findings that matter across the batch

1. Nearly every speculative-MoE speedup in #0/#1 is measured with experts resident in GPU memory (MoE-Spec, EcoSpec, Cascade) or staged from pinned host RAM over PCIe to a GPU (SpecMoEOff, SP-MoE, MoE-SpeQ, AcceptMoE, DraftExpert-CG). None compares with an LRU cache of expert footprints over an SSD. Exceptions that touch flash or SSD: S2-MoE (Jetson Orin SSD, llama.cpp, avg 2.0x vs AR with the same offload) and DraftExpert-MN (phone Flash, llama.cpp Q4_0, 1.52-1.61x). These two are the real threat to novelty and the only headline numbers that could transfer even loosely.
2. Our byte-side refutation of the union idea is consistent with the papers' own framing: naive speculation under offload loses to AR (DraftExpert Table 2: shared+top-r 1.19 vs AR 2.19 TPS; Cascade Fig 13: up to 54% slower at K=3). Published wins come from extra machinery (trained drafter, prefetch, lossy verifier expert restriction), not from the union itself.
3. P-0025 (Opportunistic Expert Activation) is misread in the catalog and in pubtech/A.md line 105. The numbers 43.0 -> 22.2 and 51.6 -> 26.5 are average activated experts per layer (Table 4), not AIME24/GPQA accuracy. Accuracy at k0=3 on Qwen3-30B barely moves (AIME 80.0 vs 80.4, GPQA 58.6 vs 60.2, Table 6). But it works by filling each token's extra experts from experts already loaded for the rest of the batch (batch 16, H100), which has no analogue at batch 1. So it is neither evidence for nor against a plain 10 -> 6 cut.
4. Prefill (#2): EStream Fig 3(a) reports 80.1-98.1% of layer-expert pairs touched at 512 tokens and Fate reports 55.9 of 60 experts per layer, supporting the screen's assumed 0.75-0.95 union and above our decode-trace proxy (0.55 at 128 tokens, 0.61 at 249). Caveat: all are 32-64-expert models, where saturation comes sooner than with 512 experts top-10.
5. Pubtech A.md labels the s1 #1 Cascade row P-0039; in TESTLIST P-0010 is Cascade for #1 and P-0039 is the s1 #5 row.
6. The largest speedup numbers in the family rows are against weak baselines: DyMoE 14.58x vs Fiddler (2.27x vs the best, Mixtral-Offloading), SpecMoEOff 17.7x vs DeepSpeed (2.1x vs MoE-Lightning), DAOP 8.20x. None is against llama.cpp with an LRU/page cache on a fast SSD, except FlashMoE (2.5x prefill vs llama.cpp, GPU 16GB).

### New prior art (not in the catalog)
Opened pages are marked (opened).
- EVICT, "Making Every Verified Token Count: Adaptive Verification for MoE Speculative Decoding" (Pan et al., arXiv 2605.00342, May 2026; opened, abstract only): truncates the draft tree to a cost-effective prefix using offline-profiled verification cost; up to 2.35x vs AR, avg 1.21x over EAGLE-3 on SGLang; GPU, no offloading found. Same mechanism as Cascade/EcoSpec. Does not change novelty; strengthens that the union cost is well known.
- SpecMoE, "Fast and Efficient MoE via Self-Assisted Speculative Decoding" (Bang, Rhu et al., KAIST, arXiv 2604.10152, Apr 2026; opened): draft built from hot experts of the target kept on the GPU, verification fetches the union once; up to 4.30x vs MoE-OnDemand, avg 2.25x when experts are offloaded to SSD; H100 + 1TB DDR5, NLLB-MoE/Mixtral/Llama-4-Scout. Threat: SSD offload with union verify is published, but on a GPU server with a big host cache.
- MoESD (Huang et al., arXiv 2505.19645, Jun 2025; opened): at moderate batch nearly all experts are already loaded each step, so verification is nearly free; up to 2.29x on Qwen2-57B-A14B (vLLM, 80GB GPUs). Says offloading would widen the benefit, analytical only. Context, not threat: regime is large-batch.
- WiSP, working-set view of MoE serving on low-resource hardware (Zhang et al., arXiv 2606.21868; PDF unreadable, abstract only via a search snippet, so details unverified): the snippet says at batch 1 PCIe transfer is the binding limit and only 44-48% of top-8 experts carry over between adjacent steps. If confirmed it supports our low-locality assumption; unverified.
- Speculating Experts (Madan et al., arXiv 2603.19289, Mar 2026; opened abstract): predicts next-layer experts from internal state, up to 14% lower TPOT vs on-demand CPU loading. Prefetch, not speculation; weakly related.
- AdapMoE (arXiv 2408.10284), DeaMoE (arXiv 2608.14385) - see #3.
- From memory (not opened): Lookahead Decoding (Fu et al. 2023), Medusa, EAGLE 1-3, Sequoia, SpecInfer, Mixtral-Offloading (Eliseev and Mazur 2023, speculative expert prefetch), MoE-Infinity, EdgeMoE, "LLM in a Flash" (Apple, Alizadeh et al. 2023), Pre-gated MoE, DeepSeek-V3 MTP. The web search found no paper combining Jacobi/n-gram lookahead with MoE expert offloading specifically.

### Memory check
Core idea (verify several tokens, pay the union of experts) is well known; Mixtral-era speculative-decoding papers already note it, and 2025-26 has about a dozen papers on it. Closest work: S2-MoE (llama.cpp + SSD + edge) and SpecMoE (SSD offload). Our variant differs in regime: CPU-only, tiny cache of 2-8 footprints, 125B with 512 experts, exact (lossless) verify. That regime is not measured by any paper read, but the pieces (union grows with the tree, naive speculation loses under offload) are published.

### Verdict
- Novelty: known (variant at best: lossless exact union in a disk-bound CPU regime with a footprint-sized cache).
- Published evidence supports a speedup in our regime: unclear. Gains exist for GPU/PCIe offload and (S2-MoE) Jetson SSD vs AR, but baselines are not an LRU-cached best path, models are 7-30B (S2-MoE largest is GPT-OSS-120B at top-4 of 128), and several methods are lossy (MoE-Spec budget, AcceptMoE restriction, S2-MoE gating).
- Our measurement: byte side refuted (LRU-relative bytes per accepted token >= 1.002 for acceptance <= 0.9; only the all-accepted k=8 cell gives 1.11x). Not contradicted by any paper read. The unmeasured piece the papers lean on, batch-k compute amortisation, remains unmeasured.
- Numbers that cannot apply to us: MoE-Spec 10-30% and EcoSpec 1.62x (H100/H200/A100, resident experts); MoE-SpeQ 2.34x/3.3x (A100, weak baseline); SpecMoEOff 2.5x (batch 500-800, 190-250GB RAM); SP-MoE 1.07-3.5x (RTX GPU + 512GB RAM); SpecExec (dense model).

### New prior art
- Same set as #0 (EVICT, SpecMoE, MoESD, AcceptMoE already counted above).
- DeaMoE: Efficient MoE Structure for Fast Small-Batch Decoding (Jin et al., arXiv 2608.14385, Aug 2026; opened abstract): a new architecture that loads 49-63% of the expert weights per step for batches of 4-64 tokens; 1.33x TPOT on an A40. Requires training a new model, so not a threat to a post-hoc idea; it does state that loading ratio returns toward baseline as the union widens, which agrees with our cold-union measurement.
- ExpertFlow (arXiv 2410.17954; found in search, not opened): token re-batching per expert gives up to 9.94% on XSUM; weak evidence.
- From memory: Lynx (dynamic batch-aware expert selection, 2024), batch-wise expert reuse in Pre-gated/ExFlow; the batch-union argument for serving is old (Switch/GShard-era).

### Memory check
"Group tokens so experts are shared" is the standard serving argument for batching; for one sequence the group is a speculation block, which is #0. Closest: Cascade, MoESD, MoE-Spec. Our variant (no draft, k consecutive decode tokens, tiny cache) is not published, but cannot be run without speculation because tokens are sequentially dependent, so it collapses into #0.

### Verdict
Novelty: known (no distinct idea beyond #0). Published evidence supports a speedup: no for batch 1 on disk; Cascade shows up to 54% slowdowns, DraftExpert shows naive drafting slower than AR. Our measurement: best gain over k=1 LRU 1.04x, drafted 0.96x; kill rule met. Not applicable to us: AcceptMoE 2.06x (restricts the verifier to resident experts, lossy, 48-slot GPU pool vs host RAM); MoE-SpeQ and SP-MoE as above.

### New prior art
- Layered prefill, "From Tokens to Layers" (Lee et al., MLSys 2026, arXiv 2510.08055; opened abstract): chunked prefill raises memory traffic up to 39% by reloading experts per chunk; layer-group scheduling cuts TTFT up to 70%. Strengthens the idea that expert reloads dominate chunked prefill; GPU serving, not disk.
- MoE-Gen, module-based batching (Xu et al., Edinburgh, arXiv 2503.09716; opened): gathers tokens in host RAM and runs each expert once on a big batch; 8-31x throughput over model-batched offload systems, Mixtral to DeepSeek-R1 671B; needs 256-512GB RAM and offline throughput. This is the nearest to "read each expert once per layer for the whole batch"; threatens novelty of expert-major prefill, but for a GPU and large host RAM.
- Mixture of Cache-Conditional Experts (Skliar et al., Qualcomm, TMLR 2025, arXiv 2412.00099; opened abstract): cache-aware routing on mobile, 2x speedup; decode-side, relevant to #3.
- Speculating Experts, SeqMoE (arXiv 2609.12978, opened abstract only, hardware unspecified), DALI, FlashOffload blog (double-buffered full-layer experts for prefill), EdgeMoE (from memory): all prefetch/overlap work, not union-by-expert.
- llama.cpp RFC #23324 (kisasexypantera94, May 2026; opened): pread-based expert pool with LRU in Metal memory for Qwen3-30B-A3B; 38.1 tok/s vanilla -> 12 tok/s with 8 slots on an M3 Pro 36GB. Practical evidence for how slow a tiny pool is; decode only.
- From memory: "LLM in a Flash" and the mmap-vs-explicit-read lever; llama.cpp users report prompt processing as the bottleneck for oversized MoE on mmap (issue/blog search; anecdotal).

### Memory check
Batching tokens per expert in prefill is standard (MegaBlocks, vLLM fused-MoE, llama.cpp mul_mat_id in later versions - the last from memory, PR #4406 itself says BS>1 was unoptimised). The new element is applying it to disk-resident experts so that each expert slice is read once: published for GPU+RAM (MoE-Gen, Fiddler, Fate, DAOP) and phone NPU+flash (EStream), not for CPU-only laptop+NVMe at 125B. The gain vs plain mmap is a read-size effect, not a sorting effect.

### Verdict
Novelty: variant (known mechanism, open regime: CPU-only, NVMe, 512 experts). Published evidence supports a speedup in our regime: yes against mmap/demand paging (EStream 2.25-27.6x TTFT vs CPU runtimes, FlashMoE 2.5x prefill vs llama.cpp), unclear vs an explicit-read best path (no paper has that baseline). Our measurement: gain vs best path 1.0x by construction, vs mmap 2.9-7x depending on assumptions; real union on OLMoE not yet measured. Numbers that cannot apply: EStream (phone NPU, Q4, 16-30B), Fate/DAOP/Fiddler (GPU, top-2 or small models), FlashMoE load 4x (start-up only).

### New prior art
- SERE, Similarity-based Expert Re-routing (Wu et al., Alibaba, ICLR 2026, arXiv 2602.07616; opened): re-routes a token's skipped experts to similar already-active experts; up to 2.0x decode, Qwen3-30B-A3B top-2 keeps 80.37 vs 82.24 avg (TPOT 44.4 -> 32.8 ms); notes direct top-K reduction without re-routing loses far more. H20 GPU, vLLM, batched QPS. Threat to novelty of "cache-aware 6-of-N", strengthens that a naive cut hurts.
- SMoE / Importance-Driven Expert Scheduling (Zhu et al., arXiv 2508.18983; opened): replaces low-importance experts with cached ones; TPOT -24% (batch 1) to -35% (batch 3); GPU 12-48GB + host RAM. Direct prior art for cache-aware substitution.
- Cache-Aware Routing / Mixture of Cache-Conditional Experts (arXiv 2412.00099; opened abstract): training-free biasing of router logits toward cached experts; 2x on mobile. Direct prior art for our "cache-aware 6-of-8".
- AdapMoE (arXiv 2408.10284; opened abstract): sensitivity-based adaptive gating, about 25% fewer activated experts, 1.35x with no accuracy loss (models/hardware not on page). Prior art for dynamic per-token k.
- Ada-K routing (arXiv 2410.10456), Elbow-Based MoE Routing (2608.04401), Distribution-Consistent Inference for Dynamic Sparse MoE (2609.09241; reports lowering k raises output variance and proposes layer-wise rescaling): found in search, not opened; cited from snippets only.
- Flash-MoE public project (see P-0022): production-style use of top-10 -> 4 on a 512-expert model on a laptop SSD; quality claims anecdotal, disputed in commentary (search results).
- From memory: Mixtral top-2 skip (Jiang et al. 2024, "Mixtral of Experts"), Top-p routing ("Harder tasks need more experts", 2024), HOBBIT (mixed-precision expert offload), Ada-K, MoE-Pruner, DeepSeek-V2/3 designs where k is trained, not cut.

### Memory check
Reducing active experts at inference is well known (since Mixtral-era skipping; adaptive-k, cache-aware routing and substitution are all 2024-26). Closest to our variant: Mixture of Cache-Conditional Experts and SMoE (cache-biased substitution) plus Flash-MoE (practitioner cut to 4 of 10 on SSD). Our variant's difference that matters: regime (SSD-bound, cache of a few footprints) and top-10 of 512 experts, where small published models (top-2 to top-8) are weak evidence on quality.

### Verdict
Novelty: known (variant only in regime and quality gate). Published evidence supports the speedup in our regime: yes for bytes and compute (arithmetic plus Flash-MoE, vllm-mlx +16.5% for 8 -> 4), unclear for quality (NAEE -3 to -8 points on Mixtral at 2 experts dropped; REAP fails outside code; SERE says naive cut is much worse; the vllm-mlx quality table is empty; OEA is not evidence either way). Our measurement: K=6 vs K=8 misses 1.46-1.52x fewer (reproduced); Amdahl ceiling 1.19x; OLMoE top-6 +8.8% perplexity, above the 5% gate. Numbers that cannot apply: OEA 39% (batch 16, per layer, H100); LExI (batch 16, vs pruning); SERE 2.0x (batched GPU); AdapMoE 1.35x (models unspecified); SliceMoE 1.81x (simulated hardware); DyMoE 14.58x (weak baseline).
