Prior art not in catalog (4 searches + remembered):
- SpecMD, Hoang et al. (Apple), arXiv 2602.03921, Feb 2026 (abstract read): benchmarks MoE cache policies, says expert access does not follow temporal locality, so LRU/LFU do poorly; proposes Least-Stale eviction (collision misses down to 85x vs LRU at 1% capacity; 88%+ hit at 5% cache on OLMoE, TTFT -34.7%). Relevant: a replacement-policy paper that does not mention ARC. Threat to novelty of "better eviction", but supports our Belady-LRU gap (0.21 at 2 footprints).
- In-Depth Analysis on Caching and Pre-fetching in MoE Offloading, Lin et al. (CMU), arXiv 2511.05814 (opened): Mixtral 8x7B, batch 1, GPUs; LFU vs LRU tok/s e.g. A6000 2.34 to 4.32, A100 3.33 to 3.64, RTX 3090 3.07 to 3.09. Small student study; policy comparison already exists; no ARC.
- ProMoE, arXiv 2410.22134 (opened): RTX 4090, 128 GB DRAM; in llama.cpp, decode 1.66x vs layer offload and 1.09x vs LRU (Table avg); 1.49x vs static. Shows that LRU is already within about 10% of a prefetching system in llama.cpp, which agrees with our small ARC gain.
- bounded-moe (Hugging Face, THISITLLM; opened): experimental llama.cpp/GGML SSD-backed expert store with bounded cache; RTX 4060 + 32 GB RAM; its own author says plain mmap is significantly faster at present. Closest public project to our regime; no numbers given.
- HOBBIT (arXiv 2411.01433) and SMoE (arXiv 2508.18983) appeared in searches; SMoE PDF unreadable (metadata only). Cache-aware routing, Skliar et al. 2412.00099, cited in our R16.
- From memory: Eliseev and Mazur, Mixtral offloading (LRU of experts, Dec 2023); Fiddler; Pre-gated MoE; ExpertFlow; AdapMoE; Klotski.

Memory check: caching experts with LRU/LFU on a per-request basis is well known (Eliseev 2023, MoE-Infinity 2024). ARC specifically has no published MoE use that I know of or found, but ARC's benefit (+0.005-0.012 hit) is tiny in our traces.

Verdict: novelty: known (variant only in policy choice: ARC unstudied). Evidence supports speedup in our regime: no. Our measurement: ARC minus LRU +0.009 hit at 8 GB (about 1% tok/s); Belady-LRU +0.21 so predictor-driven policies have headroom, which SpecMD/ProMoE address. Headline numbers that cannot apply: MoE-Infinity 3.1-16.7x (GPU baselines), DAOP 8.2x (GPU, HF Transformers), OD-MoE 3.69 tok/s (ten GPUs).

- MLX issue #3208 "RDMA weight streaming" (opened, opened 2026-03-05, closed): Phase 4 proposes on-demand expert demand-paging from a master over TB5 RDMA at about 5 GB/s, about 50 ms per expert block, author says may not be practical for real-time; measured broadcast 3.7-5.2 GB/s. Papers whose headline cannot apply: Geerling (512 GB nodes, GPU-class compute), OD-MoE (ten GPUs), Wi-Fi paper (8 GPU nodes).

- From memory: NAEE (Not All Experts are Equal, 2024), dynamic top-p routing (Huang et al., NeurIPS 2024, cited by the Elbow paper), LExI, REAP.

Memory check: top-k reduction at inference is well known and the speed gain is small unless batch compute dominates. Our regime differs: disk-bound with tiny cache, where fewer experts per token reduces bytes read, so the 8 GB serial gain (1.6-2.4x) comes mostly from cache footprint, not compute.

Verdict: novelty: known. Published evidence supports speedup in our regime: unclear (all speedups are GPU, mostly compute-bound or prefill; no SSD number), but it supports about 1.3x on the compute ceiling and a quality cost. Our measurement: ceiling lift 1.35x at top-4, 8 GB serial 1.6-2.4x, 16/32 GB 1.3-1.45x; prior OLMoE perplexity +45.6% at top-4 (gate 5%). The 5.3% Elbow and 1.42x HookMoE headline cannot apply (tiny k change; prefill).

Prior art not in catalog (4 searches, 3 opened):
- MoE-SVD, Li et al., ICML 2025 (proceedings page opened): 60% compression, about 1.5x faster inference, one V matrix shared across experts and top-k U matrices; Mixtral, Phi-3.5, DeepSeek, Qwen2 MoE. Closest to our shared-basis idea; threatens novelty of the basis structure; the 1.5x agrees with our closed-form 1.58x.
- MoBiE, arXiv 2604.06798 (snippet): shared U-Sigma per layer plus binary expert projections.
- MoLAE (appears as baseline in the MoBE table, 12-30% compression, 86-93% retention), MoE-I2 (appears among D2-MoE baselines).
- From memory: delta-compression for fine-tuned models (BitDelta, Delta-CoMe, DeltaZip), MC-SMoE, HC-SMoE.
- None of the work found reports CPU or SSD decode speed or a kernel that reads a shared basis once per layer.

Memory check: shared-base plus low-rank-delta compression of experts is well known (2025 wave). Our variant differs only in regime: bytes streamed from disk at batch 1 against a 4.3-bit baseline. Published ratios are against 16-bit weights; against 4.3 bit, fp16 factors reach break-even at rank 150 (our arithmetic), so the 50% compression claims do not carry over.

Verdict: novelty: known (method), variant for the disk-bound CPU regime. Published evidence supports a speedup in our regime: no (no decode speed on CPU; quality costs at 40-60% on every model; speedups only on GPU batch 64 of 2-12% vs other compressors). Our closed-form: r=64 stores 54% of the 4.3-bit bytes, ceiling lift 1.58x; the residual-energy measurement is still unmeasured. Numbers that cannot apply: D2-MoE 313 tok/s at batch 64, Sub-MoE 1.38x on 8 H800, MoE-SVD 1.5x (all GPU, 16-bit baselines).
