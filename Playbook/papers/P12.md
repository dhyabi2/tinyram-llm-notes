### Prior art not in the catalog (opened unless noted)
- Anthropic cookbook "speculative prompt caching" (https://platform.claude.com/cookbook/misc-speculative-prompt-caching): sends a 1-token request while the user types to warm the cache for a fixed 151k-token context; TTFT 20.87 s to 1.94 s in one run. It caches the fixed context, not the partial question. Strengthens the "warm while typing" idea but only for fixed context; weakly threatens novelty of the general trick.
- Stream2LLM, Bachkaniwala et al., MLSys 2026, arXiv 2604.16395 (opened abstract): overlaps streamed (retrieved) context with prefill, append and update modes, LCP matching to keep KV; up to 11x TTFT; GPU, prefill-decode disaggregated. Input is retrieved context, not typing. Closest published incremental-prefill system; threatens novelty of "prefill an incrementally arriving prompt", not of the disk-bound MoE regime.
- Layered prefill, Lee et al., MLSys 2026, arXiv 2510.08055 (opened): chunked prefill in MoE models adds redundant expert weight loads, memory traffic up to 39%; layer-grouped scheduling cuts TTFT up to 70%. Supports our measurement that chunking multiplies weight reads (ours: 4.3-6.0x disk bytes, a bigger factor because our cache is tiny). GPU, model and hardware not given on the abstract page.
- SeqMoE, arXiv 2609.12978 (opened): offloading predictor/cache/runtime; abstract page does not mention chunked prefill. A search summary claimed it says chunked prefill fits offloading badly; NOT confirmed on the page, treat as unverified.
- Mini-SGLang / SGLang radix cache, LMCache, Mooncake (from memory): prefix KV reuse in serving; known, not about typing.
- hearth and other disk-persistent KV proxies (search snippet only, not opened): persist KV across restarts on Apple M3 Max; confirms the KV save/restore path is cheap (0.04 s for ~3.7k tokens, vendor-style number, unverified).

### Memory check
Exact-prefix KV reuse is thoroughly known (vLLM automatic prefix caching, SGLang RadixAttention, llama.cpp cache_prompt). Cache warming ahead of the question is known for fixed context (cookbook above). I do not recall a paper that prefills the user's partially typed text in chunks. Our variant differs in the regime that matters: when every chunk pass re-reads most expert weights from disk, the chunk passes cost a large fraction of one full pass, so typing-time prefill can cost more than it saves.

### Verdict
- Novelty: variant (mechanism known; partial-typed-prompt chunking in a disk-bound MoE not found in literature).
- Published evidence supports a speedup in our regime: no. All published TTFT gains are GPU, retrieved or fixed context, and none addresses weight re-reads per chunk except Layered prefill, which says chunking hurts MoE.
- Our measurement (F.md): decode tok/s 1.0x by construction; TTFT 1.6x to 3.1x for typed prompts depending on routing model (decode proxy 2.04x, scaled-to-125B 3.07x, uniform 1.56x, and 0.64x at 5 tok/s typing under uniform routing); 0.18-0.34x if forced on a pasted prompt; disk bytes 4.3-6.0x. Screen claimed 7.8x: refuted.
- Numbers that cannot apply to us: Sarathi 2.6x/3.7x (GPU capacity), Stream2LLM 11x (GPU, retrieval), cookbook 20.87 s to 1.94 s (hosted API, 151k fixed context).

### Prior art not in the catalog
- Characterizing WebGPU Dispatch Overhead, Maczan, arXiv 2604.02344 (opened): per-dispatch API cost 24-36 us (Vulkan), 32-71 us (Metal), about 95 us total per op including Python; kernel fusion gave +53% throughput on Vulkan and none on CUDA; single-op benchmarks overestimate dispatch cost about 20x. Gives a per-op overhead range for the "50 us" row of our Amdahl table, GPU/browser only. v2 is marked withdrawn and superseded; use with care.
- Event Tensor (MLSys 2026) and megakernel work (MonoMoE, Alpha-MoE, Cohere): search snippets only, not opened. Report 5-10 us GPU launch latency and persistent single-launch MoE decode kernels. GPU only; supports "launch overhead matters on GPU", irrelevant to CPU.
- FairyFuse, arXiv 2604.20913 (search snippet only, not opened): a fused CPU kernel where removing OpenMP barriers is worth about 5% at 48 threads, per the snippet. The only CPU datapoint found on barrier cost; unverified.
- SGLang on Intel Xeon 6, LMSYS blog July 2025 (snippet only): AMX/AVX512 fused MoE kernels, 6-14x TTFT and 2-4x TPOT vs llama.cpp, self-reported. Same lever as KTransformers (kernel arithmetic), server CPUs.
- From memory: llama.cpp ggml uses a threadpool with a barrier between graph ops; CUDA graphs, torch.compile and TensorRT-LLM fuse launch sequences on GPU. Fused expert GEMMs (grouped GEMM, vLLM fused_moe) are standard.

### Memory check
Dispatch/launch overhead and fusion are well known on GPU; on CPU the analogue is thread-barrier and op-boundary cost, usually a few percent unless ops are tiny. No published number I know of shows 60% of CPU prefill is dispatch. Our idea restates a known GPU lever in a regime where the evidence points the other way.

### Verdict
- Novelty: known (fusion and AOT specialisation are standard; ik_llama.cpp already ships fused MoE CPU ops).
- Published evidence supports a speedup in our regime: no (all headline numbers are GPU, training, NPU or kernel arithmetic with AMX; none isolates CPU dispatch share).
- Our measurement (F.md): dispatch is 1-7% of decode at realistic 2-10 us per op, claim needs 60% (91 us per slice matmul); loose measured upper bound 11-30% from a tiny MoE on Apple silicon; decode at 8 GB is I/O bound so about 1.0x. Verdict refuted by arithmetic; a profiler share is still open.
- Cannot apply to us: TaxBreak launch counts and 14% latency (H100/H200), NVIDIA 8%/93% (GB200 training), KTransformers 4.62-19.74x (AMX + GPU), SGLang-Xeon 6-14x (server CPU), WebGPU +53% (Vulkan).

### Prior art not in the catalog
- DOMINO, Beurer-Kellner, Fischer, Vechev, "Guiding LLMs The Right Way", ICML 2024, arXiv 2403.06988 (abstract opened): subword-aligned constrained decoding with precomputation and speculative decoding; abstract claims near-zero overhead and in some cases almost 2x speedup over unconstrained decoding; model and hardware not on the page. Same forced-token idea as speculation; threatens novelty (known).
- Constrained Decoding with Speculative Lookaheads (CDSL), Nakshatri et al., NAACL 2025, arXiv 2412.10418 (opened): speculative lookahead for preference-aligned constrained decoding, 2.2-12.15x over CDLH. Different problem (not grammar forcing); baseline is an expensive lookahead heuristic, so the number is irrelevant to us.
- Flash-MoE write-up (insiderllm.com, 22 Mar 2026, updated Aug 2026; opened): 397B MoE with 2-bit and 4-bit experts streamed from SSD on a 48 GB M3 Max; relevant to family 24 only as a warning: 2-bit experts corrupted JSON output (tool calling unreliable), which interacts with schema-forced output. Secondary source, repository unmaintained since 19 Mar 2026.
- Outlines, Guidance, lm-format-enforcer, SGLang, vLLM/XGrammar, llama.cpp GBNF (from memory): the whole ecosystem supports grammar constraints; jump-forward/fast-forward exists in SGLang and llguidance.

### Memory check
Core idea (skip sampling when one token is legal, prefill the forced run) is well known: SGLang compressed FSM (Feb 2024), Guidance "token healing" / fast-forward (earlier), llguidance fast-forward, DOMINO speculation. Our variant differs in cost model: in a disk-bound MoE a forced run is one batched pass that touches the union of its tokens' experts, so extra bytes eat the saving; published GPU work treats a forced run as nearly free.

### Verdict
- Novelty: known (mechanism); the disk-bound MoE cost model is a variant.
- Published evidence supports a speedup in our regime: unclear to no. No source measures forced-token skipping with expert streaming; GPU numbers (2x, 1.6x, 2.5x) are for dense 7B with cheap prefill.
- Our measurement (F.md, synthetic schema outputs, not real tool-call outputs): f 0.44-0.61, mean forced run 3.3-5.4, speedup 1.1-1.5x conservative, up to 2.3x favourable, 1.0x on free text; the 3.3x claim is refuted. 0.98x at 8 GB with perfect overlap. Note the independent maskbench forced-token share of about 12.7% suggests real f can be much lower than our synthetic f, so our range may be optimistic.
- Cannot apply: LMSYS/SGLang GPU latency and throughput, XGrammar TPOT (RTX 4090), Formatron 1.5-2x (mask cost).

### Prior art not in the catalog (the most important finding of this batch)
- SliceMoE, "Bit-Sliced Expert Caching under Miss-Rate Constraints", arXiv 2512.12990 (search summary only; page not opened by me): caches MSB and LSB slices of each expert independently using Matryoshka-style truncation (AMAT) so low-bit slices can be served from a smaller footprint and more experts fit; claims below 5% miss rate, caching more low-bit experts beats fewer full-precision ones; decode latency up to 1.81x and 1.64x on DeepSeek-V2-Lite and Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B. This is the closest published match to a 2-bit base plus refinement for MoE caching and threatens novelty directly. Needs to be opened and read in full before any claim of novelty.
- HOBBIT, Tang et al., arXiv 2411.01433, Nov 2024 (abstract opened): replaces less critical cache-miss experts with low-precision versions, built on llama.cpp, on edge devices; up to 9.93x decode vs unnamed offloading baselines. Directly threatens novelty of "low-bit fallback on miss" in llama.cpp; bit widths not stated on the page.
- Bandwidth-Efficient Adaptive MoE via Low-Rank Compensation, arXiv 2512.17073, Dec 2025 (abstract opened): low-bit experts plus router-chosen low-rank compensation for the top-n of k experts, offloading from CPU memory; no numbers on the abstract. Same "refine only some experts per token" trigger as our idea, via low-rank factors instead of bit tiles.
- Matryoshka Quantization (MatQuant), Nair et al., ICML 2025, arXiv 2502.06786 (search summary only): nested int8/int4/int2, int2 up to 4% and 7% better than standard int2 with OmniQuant and QAT. Supplies the quality recipe that makes a 2-bit base viable; relevant to our "quality unmeasured" gap.
- GRINQH, arXiv 2606.23419 (search snippet only, not opened): bit-planar weights in DRAM read per input channel from activation magnitude. A different trigger; unverified.
- Flash-MoE (opened, see family 24): 397B MoE, 4-bit at about 4.36 tok/s and 2-bit at 5.74 sustained on a 48 GB M3 Max with SSD streaming; 2-bit corrupts JSON, perplexity 8.09 vs 5.51 at 4-bit (search snippet). Indirect evidence that a plain 2-bit expert is quality-limited and that streaming with a page-cache hit rate around 71% is a real working design.
- SpecPrefetch, arXiv 2607.24787 (search snippet only): cold NVMe MoE prefetch, throughput gains 6.97% cold, 15.43% slow storage. Context for the I/O-bound regime.
- From memory: Eliseev and Mazur, "Fast Inference of Mixture-of-Experts Language Models with Offloading", arXiv 2312.17238 (LRU expert cache plus mixed quantisation); MoE-Infinity, ProMoE, Mixtral-offloading, llama.cpp quantised MoE (Q2_K experts).

### Memory check
Mixed-precision experts keyed on hotness or router score is a known cluster (HOBBIT, DynaExq, SliceMoE, low-rank compensation); bit-plane/nested storage is known (Any-Precision, MatQuant, AnyBCQ). Our variant differs in two ways that matter: 6 bpw stored (96 GB vs 69 GB), and a trigger intended to be the residual norm per slice. Our own measurement finds nothing to select on (coefficient of variation 0.010), which removes the basis for the trigger and leaves the idea indistinguishable from a plain 2.2 bpw model.

### Verdict
- Novelty: known (the base-plus-refine-by-router idea is covered by HOBBIT, SliceMoE and the low-rank compensation paper; the residual-norm trigger is not found, but our measurement says it cannot work).
- Published evidence supports a speedup in our regime: unclear. HOBBIT and SliceMoE report decode gains with low-bit caching, but on GPUs or edge devices with models of 2-16B; none is a 125B 512-expert model from NVMe on 8-32 GB CPU. DynaExq's 2.73x is GPU batch 32.
- Our measurement (F.md): 8 GB serial 1.4-1.66x, overlap 1.0-1.09x; 16/32 GB about 1.0x; tiles every token 0.86-0.88x at 16/32 GB; gain equals plain 2.2 bpw; top 10% of slices hold 14% of residual energy. The 8 GB-only gain mostly comes from a 1.955x larger cache, which published work (SliceMoE miss-rate argument) also explains.
- Cannot apply: DynaExq 2.73x (A6000, batch 32, vs ExpertFlow), AnyBCQ 3.0x (A100, dense 8-14B), HOBBIT 9.93x (unnamed baselines, devices not stated on the page).
