## P-0111: Fast Inference of Mixture-of-Experts Language Models with Offloading

- Paper: Artyom Eliseev, Denis Mazur (MIPT / Yandex SDA / Researchcore), arXiv:2312.17238v1, cs.LG, 28 Dec 2023. Technical report, no venue. Read: v1 PDF, all 12 pages.
- Setup (Sec 2.4, 3.3, 4): Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct (46.7B total, experts 45.1B = 96.6%, 8 experts/layer, top-2, 32 layers). HQQ quantization; attn 4-bit, experts 2-bit (about 2.6 bit/param, 17.54 GB model) or 3-bit (21.37 GB). Hardware: free Colab T4 16 GB PCIe3; RTX 3080 Mobile 16 GB PCIe4; RTX 3060 12 GB PCIe3; A100-80GB-SXM as reference; cloud Azure T4 for 3-bit. Host RAM large enough to hold the experts (pinned). Batch size 1, sampling (no temperature), OpenAssistant conversations. LRU cache k experts per layer: k=2 for 12 GB GPUs, k=4 for 16 GB (25% / 50% of the 8 experts). Speculative loading: apply next layer's gate to the current layer's hidden state, fetch 1-2 experts. Software: PyTorch, pinned buffers, 4 shared on-device staging buffers. No SSD involved at all (CPU RAM to GPU).
- Baseline: "naive offloading (accelerate)" loads the whole layer's experts.
- Numbers (Table 2, p.7, tokens/s, batch 1; I computed ratios):

- Transfers to our regime: partly. - Contradicts our findings? No. Consistent with our pubtech C.md claim for "screen 1 #17" that this paper is a batch-1 system and that the batching sentence is only motivation. One note: the paper's per-layer LRU with k=2 equals exactly one token's top-2 footprint, i.e. at the cliff edge of what we measured; its LRU gain of 1.27-1.38x at that size comes mostly from adjacent-token reuse inside a layer, which our global-LRU cliff test (0.0 below 1 footprint, in the global cache) would not capture. Per-layer LRU of size >= top-k is not the same policy as global LRU below one footprint; our per-layer variants (D.md, per_layer_lru_hits) are the like-for-like comparison.

## P-0116: Paging the Experts: A Reproducible Characterization of Flash-Backed MoE Inference on iPhone (Routide)

- Paper: Musa Shams (independent researcher), arXiv:2609.29032v1 [cs.PF], 24 Sep 2026. Read: PDF text, all pages.
- Setup (Sec 3, 4): Qwen3.6-35B-A3B 4-bit MLX checkpoint (40 layers, 256 routed experts, top-8; 1,769,472 B = 1.6875 MiB per expert; routed experts 18.12 GB of a 20.4 GB payload). Swift/MLX runtime "Routide": positional reads (no mmap), byte-budgeted (layer, expert) cache; per-expert block holds gate+up+down projections with scales/biases contiguously, aligned to 64 KiB (27 units). Hardware: iPhone 17 Pro Max (iOS 26.5.2/26.6.1/27.0; RAM not stated in the paper; flash bandwidth not measured); Mac M1 Ultra 128 GiB for resident-reference and same-runtime controls. Greedy, batch 1, serial single-token prefill (no batched prefill). Workloads are small designed sets: five 128-token prompts for routes, one 512-token counting task, etc.
- Numbers:
  - Abstract/Sec 5.1 table (p.6), offline replay on five recorded phone routes, hit rate %: LRU 0.00 at 512 MiB, 38.58 at 576 MiB, 47.59 at 1,024 MiB; FIFO 0.00/25.66/40.38; LFU (per residency) 0.00/1.82/7.84; recency-frequency hybrid 0.00/14.65/49.83; random (5 seeds) 18.80/21.80/37.47; pinned farthest-next-use 54.49/56.93/68.23; relaxed unpinned oracle 54.73/57.13/68.31.
  - Capacity: one forward requests 320 experts (40 x 8) = 540 MiB if all miss; minimum observed reuse distance is 312 distinct experts on every trace; 512 MiB holds 303 experts, 576 MiB holds 341; "at least 313 entries needed for any LRU hit". 341 of 10,240 total experts is 3.3% of all experts.
  - Initial calibration (p.6, 8-token generation, 5 cold repetitions): 512 MiB 2.246 tok/s, 16.987 GB read; 576 MiB 3.052 tok/s, 9.709 GB; 1,024 MiB 3.173 tok/s, 8.025 GB. Ratio 576/512 = 1.36x tok/s, reads -43%.
  - Counting run, 512 tokens (p.8): decode 1.795, 1.852 tok/s (no prefetch), 2.085, 1.864 (prefetch); the two identical prefetch runs differ by 10.58% in elapsed time. iOS 27 pairs (p.9): 576 vs 512 MiB elapsed change -10.71%, -16.41%, -15.84%, +7.18%, -7.86%, -10.93% (last one thermal-flagged); logical read reduction 38.2-40.1%.
  - Prediction: a prefill confidence-0.20 predictor was right on 5,359/8,741 (61.31%) held-out checks; in the live Mac matrix at 512 MiB it issued 10,334 speculative loads, 6,223 useful and 4,111 wasted, +1.307% reads; at 576 MiB all were resident checks (no new I/O) and still lost 74 demand hits through recency refresh in an earlier build.
  - Memory: sampled iOS process footprint peaks 1.87-2.32 GiB (short prompts), 2.39-2.73 GiB (340-token prompt). Decode about 1.8-2.1 tok/s on the 512-token runs.
  - Correctness: Mac same-runtime eviction/prefetch controls match 2,560 of 2,560 tokens; phone vs resident-Python sequences disagree on all five cases (first difference at position 1-83), next-token agreement 624/640 = 97.5%.
- Catalog row check. "Pages quantized routed experts from flash, building on LLM in a flash": correct. "No expert-bundling layout found in the snippet": wrong with respect to the paper. Sec 3.1 states each expert block stores gate, up and down together with scales/biases and blocks start on 64 KiB boundaries; that is a per-expert bundle (the packaged equivalent of our "repack gate/up/down into one run"). What it does not do is cluster co-firing experts or bundle across experts, and it explicitly says it does not follow LLM-in-a-flash's neuron row-column bundling. The first pass hedged this ("paper stores gate/up/down per expert in one 64KiB-aligned block, though it claims no row-column bundling"); that hedge is correct.
- First-pass JSON check: "LRU hit 0.00% at 512MiB, 38.58% at 576MiB, 47.59% at 1024MiB; random 18.80% at 512MiB; oracle 54.49%": all correct (54.49 is pinned farthest-next-use; relaxed oracle 54.73). "decode ~1.8-2.1 tok/s": correct for the 512-token runs (the 8-token calibration is 2.2-3.2). "footprint 1.9-2.7 GiB": correct (1.87-2.73). "iPhone 17 Pro Max (RAM not stated)": confirmed.
- Conditions: iPhone unified memory, a model that is 20 GB against an app budget of a few GiB, 4-bit, 8-token to 128-token greedy runs on designed prompts, cache measured in expert payload bytes, no prefetch benefit established.
- Transfers: partly. What transfers is the mechanism and the numbers: the global-LRU cliff sits at one forward's working set (312-320 distinct experts), below which LRU hit is exactly 0 and random eviction still gets 18.8%; farthest-next-use gets 54% at the same budget. This matches our own measurement (hit 0.000 at 0.96 footprint, 0.306 at 1.0) and BigMoeOnEdge's replay (0.0% below one token cycle, offline optimum 18-48%). What does not: 256 experts/top-8 vs 512/top-10; 4 bits; Apple Silicon GPU; flash bandwidth never measured so no speed model; tokens/s improvement of 576 vs 512 MiB is real but on 5 prompts and one phone with thermal confounds.
- Quality loss: not evaluated; paper says no lossy routing modification is evaluated and sequence equivalence with the resident Python reference is not established (all five sequences diverge; token-level agreement 97.5% conditional).
- Ablation: yes for cache policy and capacity (replay grid of six budgets x six policies + oracle); prefetch isolated with a preserve-resident variant (shows the apparent prefetch benefit can be pure cache pollution/no-I/O). No layout ablation (a single layout is used), so "cache vs layout vs prefetch" is only cache vs prefetch.
- Limitations stated: small correlated workloads; serial prefill; thermal event; logical not physical I/O; no power claim; iOS version changes; cross-runtime numerical differences.
- Contradicts our findings? It contradicts one sentence in our C.md ("Paging the Experts ... shows no bundling layout"): it does use a per-expert bundle. It supports our LRU cliff, and it gives a second independent case (the effect of eviction policy at below-footprint budgets: random 18.8% and a recency-frequency hybrid 49.8% at 1,024 MiB above LRU 47.6%) that our "pin vs LRU" tests (B.md, D.md) already pointed to: below one footprint, anything but strict LRU wins.

## P-0117: BuddyMoE: Exploiting Expert Redundancy to Accelerate Memory-Constrained Mixture-of-Experts Inference

- Paper: Yun Wang, Lingyun Yang, Senhao Yu, Yixiao Wang, Ruixing Li, Zhixiang Wei, James Yen, Zhengwei Qi (Shanghai Jiao Tong University), arXiv:2511.10054v1 [cs.LG], 13 Nov 2025. PDF metadata says arXiv GenPDF; the PPoPP'26 venue in the first pass is not printed in the text I read (unverified). Read: PDF (layout extraction scrambled two columns, so I re-extracted without -layout for the tables).

# Results that change what we should believe about our own measurements

- Our pubtech/C.md line "Paging the Experts shows no bundling layout" is wrong (it uses a per-expert 64 KiB-aligned gate/up/down bundle). Our pubtech/B.md summary of Edge0 as "naive on-demand streaming ineffective" is not what the paper shows (19.9 vs 20.4 tok/s on 24 GB).
- Three independent measurements (ours, Routide's replay, BigMoeOnEdge's replay) agree that global LRU hits exactly 0 below one token's working set; random and farthest-next-use do not. Our "pin only wins below one footprint" finding is therefore not an artefact of our traces.
- BigMoeOnEdge's public measurements of the same 125B/512/top-10 model class: 7-9 tok/s from flash on a 16 GB Apple laptop (README only, no run data found) and 19-21 on a 16 GB GPU + 32 GB box, at 2-bit. Our modelled CPU ceiling of 4.57 tok/s at 4.3 bit does not bound 2-bit GPU-assisted runs, and the competitor result at the exact model class must be treated as existing prior art for "stream the 125B from flash on a 16 GB machine".
- Quality bound for the cache-aware substitution lever (B.md "quality unmodelled"): about 25-28% of selections rerouted gave +0.9% to +3.9% perplexity, -4/100 tinyMMLU, equal HumanEval-50 on Qwen3.6-35B; 42-46% rerouted gave +21% to +27% perplexity. At the same selection-change rates our modelled hit gains (0.56 -> 0.78 at 24%) look reachable, but on a 256-expert model.
- Their measurement that read-ahead/prediction loses when flash bandwidth is saturated (spec-2 -21%, predict-prefetch 9.5 vs 14.0, route-ahead 9.4) is consistent with our overlap analysis and counters Edge0's +80% unless the disk is idle (Edge0: disk at most 12% of step).
