### Prior art we may have missed (#20)
- Neuralink (earlier titled Ripple), "Fast LLM Inference on Smartphones with Neuron Co-Activation Linking", arXiv 2410.19274 v3, ASPLOS '25. OPENED. Offline greedy ordering of neurons in flash by co-activation, online gap merging ("access collapse") and a cache admission policy that avoids fragmenting reads. 7 dense models, OnePlus phones, 1.49x average over the state of the art, 2.37x over llama.cpp. No MoE. THREATENS novelty of the idea in general (layout by co-activation for flash), not its MoE form.
- PowerInfer-2 (arXiv 2406.06282). SEARCH SNIPPET ONLY. Reported to argue that after removing hot neurons co-activation probability falls below 20%, so bundling is ineffective. Supports our refutation.
- SGLang "SSD Expert Pack" (lmsys blog 29 Aug 2026). OPENED. Stores each (layer, expert) as one contiguous 4 KB-aligned block, O_DIRECT reads, GPU byte-budget cache; RTX 5090, 32 GB RAM. Not co-activation, but the same contiguous-per-expert layout as our repack. Hit rates 54%/46% (DeepSeek-V4-Flash) and 17%/23% (Kimi-K3); 1.7-2.0 GB (DeepSeek) and 22-28 GB (Kimi) of SSD traffic per token.
- Flash-MoE (blog, 23 Mar 2026). OPENED. Qwen3.5-397B-A17B on M3 Max 48 GB, parallel pread(), 4.36 tok/s, OS page cache about 71% hit, no layout work. Shows pread-without-custom-cache works at 17.5 GB/s disk.
- Edge0 (arXiv 2609.18063). OPENED. SSD-resident 35B MoE with a trained prerouter, 20.4 tok/s on 24 GB Mac mini; requires a trained prerouter and LoRA, quality drop of 4 points average.
- FloE (arXiv 2505.05950). SEARCH SNIPPET ONLY. Compact layout of related slices for larger transfers, in DRAM to GPU.
- From memory (not searched/opened): PowerInfer, Deja Vu, EdgeMoE (expert-wise SSD offload, 2023), llama.cpp `--n-cpu-moe` and mmap behaviour.

Memory check: co-activation layout in flash is known for dense neurons (Neuralink; LLM-in-a-flash negative result). For MoE, mbolt (2026) is the direct prior art for exactly our idea. Our variant differs in: held-out evaluation, post-cache (miss-only) run lengths, and a tiny cache of 2-8 footprints on 125B/512 experts; that part is not in mbolt's README (it reports no held-out-after-cache table).

Verdict: novelty = known (mbolt implements it; Neuralink for dense). Published evidence supports the speedup in our regime: unclear to weak. mbolt's own table is consistent with a small reorder-only gain (about 5%); its 1.55-1.63x is mostly prefetch plus interleave versus mmap. Our measurement: 1.045x serial / 1.073x overlap at 8 GB, 1.003x at 16/32 GB; held-out run length 1.35 (128 experts), 1.58 on OLMoE. No headline here applies unmodified: baseline is mmap stock llama.cpp, not explicit reads plus LRU.

### Prior art (#21)
- MoE-Spec, arXiv 2602.16052 (Meta). OPENED. Verification loads the union of experts across the draft tree; on OLMoE a 127-token tree touches 54 of 64 experts. Caps experts at verification; 10-30% over EAGLE-3; A100. Confirms our "bytes ratio up to 4x above K=1" in direction.
- DraftExpert, arXiv 2607.24434 (single author, Jul 2026). OPENED. Trained draft expert per layer plus prefetch; DeepSeek-V2-Lite and Moonlight-16B-A3B on RTX 4090 and on a Snapdragon 8 Elite phone with experts staged from flash (Q4_0, llama.cpp). Average 1.45x over AR (1.52x and 1.61x on the phone). CLOSEST to our regime (flash-staged experts, llama.cpp), but the gain is via prefetch with a trained draft, 16B-class models, and the phone has an NPU. Threatens novelty of "draft-driven prefetch from flash".
- Specter/MoE-SpeQ (above); EcoSpec (arXiv 2607.12696, up to 1.62x), AcceptMoE (2608.02989), EVICT (2605.00342, up to 2.35x): SEARCH SNIPPETS ONLY.
- From memory: Leviathan et al. and Chen et al. 2022-23 speculative decoding; Medusa, EAGLE; SpecMoEOff; Lookahead/n-gram lookup in llama.cpp. All well known.

Memory check: speculative decoding is textbook; its interaction with MoE expert union growth is documented (MoE-Spec). Using the draft only to prefetch experts (SpeQ, DraftExpert) is published on GPUs and one phone. Our variant (verify cost on disk-bound CPU with 2-8 footprint cache) differs in regime but the conclusion (more bytes per accepted token above K=1) is already in MoE-Spec's expert-union data.

Verdict: novelty = known. Published support for a speedup in our regime: no for the fewer-bytes mechanism; unclear for the prefetch variant (all published gains are GPU/PCIe or a phone with trained draft). Our measurement: bytes ratio 0.97-1.00 at K=1, up to 4.21 above; flat-compute speedup never above 0.985; llama-bench shows no cheap small batches (batch-8 per-token cost 0.70 of decode, OLMoE, noisy).

### Prior art (#2 #0)
- Layered prefill, arXiv 2510.08055 (SNU, Oct 2025). OPENED. Layer-wise instead of token-wise prefill scheduling to avoid reloading MoE weights per chunk; H100s, Qwen3-30B-A3B and GPT-OSS-20B; up to 70% lower TTFT; chunked prefill adds up to 39% extra memory traffic. Strengthens our assumption that layer-major prefill is the right design; threatens novelty of layer-major prefill (known).
- SeqMoE (arXiv 2609.12978) argues chunked prefill forces repeated expert transfers: SEARCH SNIPPET ONLY.
- SGLang SSD Expert Pack (above): prefill 2.28-3.39x (DeepSeek) and 5.8-6.96x (Kimi) over Ollama / llama.cpp on a 32 GB RTX 5090 box; cache misses block the MoE step.
- EStream (arXiv 2609.06551, MoE prefill on mobile NPUs): title and authors only, body unreadable. llm.npu (arXiv 2407.05858), HeteroLLM, "When NPUs Are Not Always Faster" (2605.27435): SEARCH SNIPPETS ONLY.
- From memory: Fiddler, KTransformers (CPU/GPU hybrid MoE), llama.cpp batch prefill; Apple Neural Engine MoE work is new (NPUMoE).

Memory check: layer-major batched prefill and NPU prefill offload are both known; NPUMoE is the first Apple NPU MoE paper I see. Our contribution is the arithmetic that prefill of ~500 tokens on 69 GB is I/O-bound (28.8 / 22.3 / 44.2 s versus 19.3 s compute), a regime none of the compute-focused papers address.

Verdict: novelty = known. Published support for a speedup from the co-processor in our regime: no (every NPU/GPU gain is measured with the model resident in memory; the one disk-streaming datapoint, Deltafin's 6.3 min TTFT, is limited by repeated reads, not by compute). Our measurement: overlapped TTFT gain exactly 1.0x at 500 tokens; break-even 576-1146 tokens; 2.7-2.9x at 2000-8000 tokens with a hypothetical 900 GFLOP/s device; coverage disagreement (55-66% of experts after 200 tokens on 128 experts) could change that, unmeasured at 125B.

### Prior art (#2 #1)
- No paper found that runs MoE expert GEMV inside an SSD (searches describe it as largely unexplored). InstInfer's own remark (CSD compute 2-3 orders weaker than GPUs, from a search snippet) supports skepticism.
- Edge0, SGLang Expert Pack, Flash-MoE (above): the practical competition is simply fast NVMe streaming (17.5 GB/s on an M3 Max).
- SSD Offloading for MoE Weights Considered Harmful in Energy Efficiency (arXiv 2508.06978, SNU). OPENED, modeled H100 nodes: DeepSeek-R1 uses 4.9x per-token energy versus HBM and 3.1x versus CPU-memory offload; SSD reads about 80% of energy. Relevant to CSD (it saves energy only if reads stay inside the drive).
- DASH (arXiv 2608.14333, high-bandwidth flash): search snippet only.
- From memory: SmartSSD and Samsung/Xilinx CSD research papers (e.g. SmartSSD, 2020); Mutlu-group NDP work.

Memory check: computational storage for LLM weights is thin in the literature; attention/KV and generic NDP dominate. Our variant is open as a concept but irrelevant for the target (laptops have no CSD slots).

Verdict: novelty = open (no one has tried it), but practically moot. Published support for a speedup in our regime: no. Our measurement: ideal pair 2.08x serial / 1.08x overlapped at 8 GB, 1.0x at 16/32 GB; whole-expert placement imbalance 24-27% so about 3.87 tok/s; two plain striped NVMe give the same 4.78 vs 4.58 ceiling.

## Cross-cutting findings
1. mbolt's published table decomposes into prefetch (1.31x), reorder (+5%), interleave (+18% on top); the reorder-only part agrees with our 1.045x measurement, which is the best external confirmation in this batch.
2. Catalog items to correct: P-0131 url (truncated), P-0125 title (now Specter; 2.34x is the v1 abstract), P-0123 speedup range, P-0132 "storage hierarchy" (compressed GPU + DRAM).
3. Every headline speedup in this batch is on a GPU, a phone or a simulator, with the model in DRAM; none is CPU-only, batch 1, NVMe-streamed at 8-32 GB.
4. Unread parts: Specter v3 (39k chars), NPUMoE (3k), FluxMoE (18k), InstInfer (12k), Conduit (98k). Papers whose PDFs returned binary (2312.11514, 2511.14102, 2604.18788, 2410.22134, 2409.04992, 2601.17633) were read through the HTML/abs versions instead.
