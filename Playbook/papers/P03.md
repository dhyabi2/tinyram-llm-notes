# P03: published-paper check, screen 1 families #8, #9, #10, #11

Date of check: 2026-10-29. Method: WebFetch of each catalog url (abstract page, then HTML/PDF when the first page lacked results), plus 15 WebSearch queries and fetches of the most relevant new hits. Compressed-PDF fetches returned no text; where only an abstract page was readable, the table says so. Facts not read on a page are marked "from memory" or "unverified". Our own numbers come from pubtech/B.md (Qwen3-30B-A3B decode traces as proxy, no model forward pass).

Counts for this batch: 23 published-method rows. Opened with usable content: 22 (abstract-only: P-0062, P-0075). Unverified: 1 (P-0058). Summaries judged wrong: 1 (P-0075). Partly accurate: 7 (P-0057, P-0063, P-0064, P-0069, P-0071, P-0072, P-0078). Accurate: 14.

Global transfer note: almost every paper below runs on a discrete GPU (3090/4090/A100/A40/T4) with the experts in host RAM over PCIe, often at batch >1 and with a cache of 25-50% of the model. Our regime is CPU-only, 8-32 GB RAM, experts streamed from NVMe, batch 1, 2-8 footprint cache. None of the headline speedups transfer directly.

### Prior art not in the catalog (family #8)
- **Mixture of Cache-Conditional Experts for Efficient Mobile Device Inference** (Skliar et al., Qualcomm; arXiv 2412.00099, 27 Nov 2024; opened HTML). Training-free cache prior added to router logits (lambda x running logit range; top-J always kept; unmodified logits still used for weights), llama.cpp on the CPU of Snapdragon phones with 12/16 GB, batch 1. Miss rate on WikiText LRU -> Prior: Qwen1.5-MoE 35% -> 7%, DeepSeek-V2-Lite 28% -> 3%, Phi-3.5-MoE 22% -> 9%, Mixtral 40% -> 21%; up to 2x token throughput vs LRU; no accuracy loss on MMLU/GSM8K reported. THREATENS novelty of the logit-bias half of #8 strongly (same regime: CPU, llama.cpp, batch 1, memory-limited) and supports the plausibility of our bias numbers. Does not do a static pin.
- **Enabling MoE on the Edge via Importance-Driven Expert Scheduling** (Zhu et al.; arXiv 2508.18983, 26 Aug 2025; opened). Substitutes low-score uncached experts with cached similar-score ones; GPUs 12-48 GB; TPOT -24% at batch 1 (-35% at batch 3), TTFT -23%; "nearly lossless" at thresholds 0.25-0.35. Threatens (substitution is the same lever).
- **Cacheable by Design? Training MoE Routers for Locality ... A Pre-Registered Negative Result** (Suram; arXiv 2608.18261, 18 Aug 2026; abstract read). Qwen3-235B Q4_K_M (134 GB) on an 8 GB GPU: 0.44 tok/s warm, bandwidth-bound; Qwen3-30B: LRU holding 13.4% of experts serves 66% of requests; training for locality gives up to 60% miss reduction with 99% static-pin hit but fails a 1% perplexity gate; training-free rerouting + trained locality gives ~80% miss reduction at <=3.4% perplexity cost. Closest regime to ours; strengthens the "bias costs quality" point and gives a numeric cost.
- **Mixture-of-experts expert-substitution in SpecMD (above) and MixtureCache** (Skliar et al. 2025 is the same line as 2412.00099).
- **Who Should Own the Expert Cache? Kernel-Managed Tiering for Trillion-Parameter MoE Inference** (arXiv 2608.12103, 12 Aug 2026; abstract read). Kernel recency (OS page cache) matches an oracle static-frequency policy at equal memory; oracle-pinned arena only 1.09-1.11x faster in replay; a static table degrades under domain shift while recency stays stable. Strongly supports our finding that a static pin does not beat LRU, and "let the page cache do it".
- **Flash-MoE** (Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, 512 experts/layer, on M3 Max 48 GB; secondary write-up lilting.ch). Experts streamed by parallel pread from SSD at 17.5 GB/s, 4.36 tok/s at 4-bit with 4 active experts; relies on OS page cache (~71% hit), mmap 5x slower, prefetch hurt. A project write-up, not a paper; the nearest working system to our target (Apple SSD, 512 experts). Threatens novelty of "stream from NVMe on a laptop", strengthens the premise.
- Reproducible Evaluation of MoE Expert Caching (arXiv 2608.07911, Aug 2026; abstract read): offline-optimal gap stays 44-46%; causal predictors recover little; recency policies inflated 27-29% under inconsistent replay. Caveat for our own trace simulations: check replay semantics.

### Memory check (#8)
From memory: cache-conditional / cache-aware routing is well known (Skliar et al. 2024; Mixtral-Offloading, Eliseev & Mazur 2023, LRU of experts; pinned hot experts appear in Pre-gated/EdgeMoE style work). Closest published work: 2412.00099. Our variant differs only in regime (512 experts, 125B, NVMe-bound, 2-8 footprint cache) and in combining a static pin with the bias.

### Verdict (#8)
Novelty: known (variant at best: bias is published, static pin is published as weak). Published evidence supports speedup in our regime: unclear to partly yes for the bias (2412.00099 reports up to 2x on a phone CPU at batch 1 with no measured accuracy loss; a 2026 negative result reports a 3.4% perplexity cost for reroute+trained locality), no for a static pin (2608.12103, 2608.18261, our own run). Our measurement (B.md): bias adds 1.37x serial / 1.08x overlap at 8 GB over LRU, ~nothing at 16/32 GB; static pin is 0.76x of LRU; quality unmeasured. Not transferable: 2.8-3.0x (P-0058, unverified, GPU), 2x on phone (smaller models, different cache fraction).

### Prior art not in the catalog (family #9)
- **Layered prefill** (MLSys 2026 paper c0f460c6...; only a search snippet; PDF not readable): groups layers into contiguous blocks and interleaves prefill with decode to avoid repeated MoE weight loads from chunked prefill, TTFT up to -70% vs chunked prefill (snippet, unverified). Same observation as #9 (weights re-read per chunk), different fix, GPU serving.
- **Fiddler** (arXiv 2402.07033, ICLR 2025; search result): CPU-GPU orchestration; long prefill 1.30x, uses a latency model choosing CPU compute vs transfer. Relevant to CPU-bound prefill. Snippet-level only.
- **MoE-Gen** (arXiv 2503.09716, ICML 2025 MLSys track; snippet): module-based batching on one GPU, 7.7-11x throughput. Shows large-batch prefill principle; GPU.
- **Fate** (P-0069) reports prefill speedup up to 4.5x over load-on-demand: prefill is I/O-intensive (inputs 128-512 tokens). Supports that prefill is load-bound.
- **Flash-MoE** (see #8): no prefill details.
- From memory: "LLM in a Flash" (Apple, 2023) for read-size/bundling; llama.cpp batched prefill already evaluates each expert tensor once per ubatch via MUL_MAT_ID, so expert-major is the default inside a ubatch (not verified here).

### Memory check (#9)
From memory: loading each expert once per layer for all tokens is what batched MoE kernels do, and what ubatch-512 does in llama.cpp; layer-wise streaming for prefill is common in offloading engines (DeepSpeed ZeRO-Inference, FlexGen). Our variant matters only if the stock path really faults experts at 0.34 GB/s; our I/O run (shared, noisy) showed mmap-copy 1.8 GB/s vs pread 2.8 GB/s, i.e. ~2.3x not 7.6x.

### Verdict (#9)
Novelty: known (variant: the gain is a read-size/overlap effect). Published evidence supports speedup in our regime: unclear (papers show prefill is load-bound and chunked prefill re-reads experts, all on GPU/host-RAM; the Reddit run shows ubatch raises prefill 5.5x on GPU). Our measurement: arithmetic reproduces (222 s -> 29 s needs mmap 0.34 GB/s), real gain 2.3-3x if mmap is ~1 GB/s; unmeasured: real prefill coverage and stock prefill rate. Not transferable: ExpertFlow 2-10x (batch 32, GPU), MoE-Lightning 10.3x (batch throughput), FineMoE TTFT -53-74% (6 GPUs).

### Prior art not in the catalog (family #10)
- **SliceMoE: Bit-Sliced Expert Caching under Miss-Rate Constraints** (arXiv 2512.12990; search snippet only, PDF not opened): says high-gating experts in prefill stay important in early decode and proposes prefill-aware cache initialisation. Directly the same idea as #10 (prefill -> warm cache). Novelty threat for "pin after prefill"; need to read it.
- **Qwen3.6-35B MoE on M1 Max: pre-warming hot experts didn't beat the mmap cache** (lilting.ch, blog; snippet only): per-prompt hot experts cover ~60% of 256 experts per layer over 150 tokens; pre-warming did not beat the OS page cache. Consistent with our negative result.
- **Who Should Own the Expert Cache?** (arXiv 2608.12103): see #8. Static frequency pin = page-cache recency at equal memory; pin degrades under domain shift. Strengthens drop verdict.
- **Cacheable by Design?** (arXiv 2608.18261): LRU at 13.4% of experts serves 66%; strengthens.
- **Reproducible Evaluation of MoE Expert Caching** (arXiv 2608.07911): causal predictors recover little of the offline-optimal gap (44-46%).
- **DuoServe-MoE** (arXiv 2509.07379; snippet): separates prefill and decode strategies.
- From memory: EdgeMoE (2023) pinning hot experts, Pre-gated MoE (2023), HOBBIT (2024, mixed precision), AdapMoE. Not read here.

### Memory check (#10)
From memory: per-request expert caching (MoE-Infinity), prefill-informed cache warm-up (SliceMoE per search snippet), and static hot-expert pinning are all known. Our variant differs in the regime (cache of 2-8 footprints, where a footprint exceeds the whole cache) but our own proxy shows LRU beats pin at every share >=10% (B.md).

### Verdict (#10)
Novelty: known. Published evidence supports a gain in our regime: no (published pin/prefill-warm work reports gains over weak baselines on GPU; the best-controlled comparisons, 2608.12103 and our B.md run, find pin <= LRU). Our measurement: pin below LRU at every share >= 10% on all three traces; hybrid minus LRU -0.003 at 36% (kill threshold +0.05). Not transferable: ProMoE 2.2x/2.07x, DALI 3.97x, Fate 99%, FlashMoE 2.6x (all GPU + big host RAM or large caches), MoE-Infinity 3.1-16.7x. Unmeasured: real prefill routing as predictor.

### Prior art not in the catalog (family #11)
- **ExFold: Unified Expert Folding for Training-Free MoE Prefill-Decode Acceleration** (Wu et al.; arXiv 2608.24938, 24 Aug 2026; abstract read). Training-free; reduces active experts per token in prefill (token-level Top-K folding) with offline-calibrated scalar projectors that fold excluded experts into kept ones; Qwen3-30B-A3B top-8 -> top-4: TTFT up to 1.41x; average score 69.04 (top-8), 66.64 (direct top-4), 69.26 (ExFold-P4). Also GLM-4.5-Air and DeepSeek-V4-Flash; vLLM + Triton, GPU unnamed. THREATENS novelty of #11 (prefill-only top-k reduction is published, with a quality fix) and shows direct top-4 costs ~2.4 average-score points on a 128-expert model.
- **Opportunistic Expert Activation: Batch-Aware Expert Routing for Faster Decode Without Retraining** (arXiv 2511.02237; snippet only): decode-side, batch union.
- **Flash-MoE** (Qwen3.5-397B-A17B on M3 Max): runs with 4 active experts instead of 10 per token to cut SSD reads (4.36 tok/s at 4-bit); the page I read did not give a k=4 vs 10 quality comparison. Secondary write-up; the quality claim ("no measurable loss") came only from a search summary, unverified.
- **Mixture of Cache-Conditional Experts** and **Importance-Driven Expert Scheduling** (see #8) are alternatives for cutting reads in decode.
- From memory: NAEE (Lu et al. 2024) dynamic skipping, Dynamic-MoE, top-p routing (DynMoE), AdapMoE (adaptive gating). Not read.

### Memory check (#11)
From memory: reducing top-k at inference, adaptive/dynamic gating and expert skipping are well known; reduced top-k in prefill specifically is documented in Lynx (prefill is sensitive) and ExFold (prefill top-4 with folding). Our variant differs only in why (cut NVMe reads) and in the geometry (512 experts, top-10). The key regime fact: the screen's arithmetic shows near-uniform 512-expert routing touches almost every expert in a 500-token prefill, so cutting k does not cut distinct experts read (B.md: 1.02x overlapped). That regime point is not covered by any paper above.

### Verdict (#11)
Novelty: known. Published evidence supports a speedup in our regime: no. Papers show prefill gains of 1.15x (Alloc-MoE, compute-bound GPU) and 1.41x (ExFold) when experts are GPU resident; none measures an I/O-bound prefill with 512 experts. Quality: P-0079 on the exact geometry says halving costs 0.55 MMLU (tuned) to 2.1 (plain); P-0078 and P-0076 show prefill and k=4 are quality sensitive. Our measurement: TTFT gain 1.02x (overlapped) to 1.13x (serial) at uniform 125B routing; 1.33x only if 512-expert routing were as skewed as the 30B decode traces (unverified). Not transferable: Lynx 1.55x (batch 8-64), SMIDT 1.2-3.1x (pipeline GPUs), Alloc-MoE 1.15x/1.34x (H100, batch 8).
