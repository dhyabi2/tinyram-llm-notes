### Memory check (#12)
Core idea (hot experts resident on the fast tier, rest streamed) is well known: Mixtral-Offloading (2023), Fiddler, MoE-Infinity, HOBBIT, kTransformers, now mainline llama.cpp `--moe-cache-mib`. Closest published work is P-0082 for the mechanism and Mixtral-Offloading for the small-cache regime. Our variant differs only in the regime (NVMe not host RAM as the slow tier; 125B/512 experts). No paper opened measures a GPU tier backed by NVMe at 512 experts.

### Verdict (#12)
Novelty: known. Published evidence supports the speedup in our regime: no (every speedup is with host RAM as slow tier and a GPU; the only NVMe-backed numbers, HOBBIT Jetson and SGLang Expert Pack, are small models or very slow baselines). Our measurement: hit rate 0.79-0.85 at 29% holds in simulation (supports 8.7-12 tok/s I/O bound), GPU compute unmeasured. Cannot apply to us: all 4090/5090/A6000/A100 headline speedups, the 2.2x at 89% hit (needs ~19 GB cache plus 256 GB host RAM), HybriMoE 1.7x (baseline kTransformers).

### Memory check (#13)
Reducing activated experts at inference is well known (Mixtral top-1 vs top-2 studies, dynamic routing, expert skipping/Ada-K, from memory). What the catalog's family adds is the I/O argument: with experts on disk, bytes per token fall roughly with k. That argument is stated explicitly only in BigMoeOnEdge among sources opened. The TTFT/prefill angle (our measured 1.0-1.1x) is not supported by any source opened; MoDES's 2.16x prefill is GPU-resident compute and VLM-specific.

### Verdict (#13)
Novelty: known (technique), variant (disk-bound framing, and prefill-by-top-4 is specifically weak). Published evidence supports the speedup in our regime: unclear for decode (BigMoeOnEdge +68% for k4 to k2 on a phone supports direction; quality effect unquantified and the test was throttled/confounded); no for prefill. Our measurement: prefill 1.0-1.1x, decode 1.47-1.74x with unmeasured quality cost (+45.6% perplexity at k=4 on OLMoE). Not applicable to us: MoDES 2.16x/1.26x (GPU, VLM), LExI throughput (H100, batch 16), KT prefill 500-1,200 tok/s (AMX/GPU).

### Memory check (#14)
Contiguous layout for flash reads is well known (Apple 2023; SGLang Expert Pack 2026 for experts specifically). An expert-granular offline repack with O_DIRECT is now published for GPU-cache systems. Our variant (4 KiB-aligned extent per expert for CPU inference on 8-32 GB machines, with a tiny cache) differs only in regime; the SGLang page itself states the layout rationale (GGUF is tensor-oriented), so layout novelty is gone.

### Verdict (#14)
Novelty: known (layout), variant (CPU, tiny cache regime). Published evidence for the speedup in our regime: unclear (no source isolates repack; BigMoeOnEdge shows mmap pathologies of 25x but from dense-weight eviction; Jetson shows 1.5x over mmap with slot cache + pread). Our measurement: layout fact confirmed (114 MB gate/up separation, no 4 KiB alignment), mmap-vs-explicit gap only 1.5-2.4x measured, one-extent vs three-read 1.38x at 8 GB. Not applicable: SGLang 6.9x (GPU cache, slow baselines), Flash-MoE (17.5 GB/s SSD, 35 GB page cache), 2-bit quality.

### Memory check (#15)
"Read experts on demand with large aligned reads" is the baseline design of every expert-streaming system (Flash-MoE, Jetson fork, RFC #23324). Removing all caching is not a published recommendation; the sources opened either keep a slot cache or lean on the OS page cache.

### Verdict (#15)
Novelty: known. Published evidence supports the speedup in our regime: partly for reading in big chunks (Apple paper, Jetson fork), no for removing the cache (Flash-MoE relied on OS cache; our own kill rule fired). Our measurement: 2.5-3.1x over plain mmap but 0.60x of the cached explicit path. Not applicable: Edge0 20.4 vs 3.9 tok/s (trained prerouter; baseline is resident); LLM in a flash 20x GPU figure (neuron sparsity, GPU); mmap-thrashing claims of P-0101 (not on the page).
