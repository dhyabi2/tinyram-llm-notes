- Transfer: no. Both dense models, GPU/TPU, QAT or calibrated GPTQ; speedups are vs BF16 weights over HBM; no MoE, no disk. What transfers: evidence that a nested format needs joint training, and that the nested low slice (int2/3) is much worse than a dedicated model at the same bits.
- Quality loss: above. Ablation: MatQuant has loss-weighting ablations (Tables 4-5), MatGPTQ lambda ablation (Tables 6-7); none about caching.
- Limitations stated: MatQuant notes hardware supports only int8/int4 natively (so int2 needs custom kernels); MatGPTQ kernels fall behind at larger batch.
- Vs our findings: supports D.md's statement that post-hoc nesting is poor and that the bytes replay (1.43-1.52x) is independent of training but quality is not.

## Per-family sections

### Family screen 2 #8 (8-dim VQ at ~1.3 bpw, LUT dot products)
Further prior art (read at the depth stated):
- KBVQ-MoE (arXiv 2602.11184v2, ICLR 2026, Zukang Xu et al.): VQ for MoE with KLT-guided SVD sharing of redundant components across experts and bias correction; abstract says 3-bit Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B avg 67.99 vs FP16 68.07 (search snippet; abstract skimmed, tables not read in detail). Closest published VQ-for-MoE; needs ~3 bit.
- MoQE (arXiv 2310.02410, Kim, Fahim, Awadalla, Microsoft): 2-bit expert-only weight quantisation, size -79.6%, 1.24x speedup on A100; read abstract/tables list (machine-translation MoE, not LLM chat models).
- QMoE (2310.16795): sub-1-bit on a 1.6T MoE with dictionary coding; skimmed header only.
- AQLM (2401.06118) and QuIP#: additive and lattice VQ at ~2 bit; compared in VPTQ and GPTVQ tables (AQLM 2.02 bit 7B: ppl 6.64, 19.4 tok/s; QuIP# 4.4 tok/s).
- EdgeMoE (2308.14352, Jetson TX2 + Raspberry Pi 4B CPU, per-expert INT2/4/8 with SSD preload, 2.64-3.03x vs IO-EXP, 1.19-2.12x vs IO-QEXP, <=2% accuracy loss claimed): this is the nearest published CPU + SSD + low-bit experts system (skimmed: abstract, Sec on speedups).
- Also found by search (not opened in full): TriS-MoE (sub-2-bit MoE), BitsMoE (spectral shared basis), XFP (Qwen3.5-397B-A17B codebooks).
What is new in our variant: I found no paper that tests a single 8-dim, 256-word (1.0 bpw plus codebook) plain k-means VQ with table-lookup dot products for streamed MoE experts on CPU. The closest cells are VPTQ Table 10 (v=4, k=256, 2.01 bit: ppl 7.21 vs 4.57 on dense 13B without fine-tune), GPTVQ (<=6-bit indices, 2D/4D, 2.1-3.1 bpv, mobile-CPU LUT, +10% over INT4) and KBVQ-MoE (3 bit). The published evidence says: the quality at 1 bpw is not reached, CPU LUT decode works but gives about +10% vs INT4 when memory bound by DRAM, and sub-3-bit VQ needs Hessian weighting and fine-tuning. So "novel" only in regime (NVMe-bound, 1 bpw), where our own measurement (rel. error 0.58 vs 0.33 for Q2_K-like) already refutes it.

### Family screen 2 #9 (2-bit base + 2-bit residual for top-ranked experts, cache)
Further prior art:
- HOBBIT (arXiv 2411.01433, Peng Tang, Jiacheng Liu et al., SJTU/CUHK, Nov 2024; read in full): replaces cache-miss experts of low importance (score from gate weight x output norm; ~67% kept high precision, ~30% low in their statistics) with a low-precision copy (int4/int2 of an int8 or fp16 expert), loads costs scale with bits, mixed-precision-aware cache (LRU/LFU/LHU/FLD), prefetch; built on llama.cpp, Jetson AGX Orin 32 GB and RTX 4090, batch 1, SSD/CPU memory. Numbers: up to 9.93x decode vs MoE-Infinity (Phi-MoE, Orin), 13.0x/18.9x vs llama.cpp (Orin, mmap page faults), 3.21x/3.29x vs MoE-Offloading; ablation: dynamic precision loading alone gives 1.19x-1.57x; accuracy Table 3: Mixtral Float16 GSM8K 0.52 vs Float16+Int4 0.51; Int8 0.52 vs Int8+Int2 0.51 (drop <=1 point). This is the closest paper to our s2_9: same idea (substitute lower-precision experts on a miss, importance-ranked) at batch 1 with SSD and llama.cpp, but with separate low-precision copies instead of nested residual bytes, GPU-assisted, 8-16 experts. It threatens the novelty of "cheap precision for non-top experts" and also gives measured effect (1.2-1.6x) in the same regime family.
- EdgeMoE (2308.14352): per-expert bit-widths, SSD, preload on CPU/GPU edge SoCs (skimmed).
- Any-Precision LLM (2402.10517, Park et al.): incremental bit-plane upscaling from a 3-bit seed model; dense; kernels only (headers read).
- NestQuant (Xie et al., 2025) cited by MatGPTQ as nested integers for on-device; not opened.
What is new in our variant: base + residual where the residual is fetched only for the router's top-ranked experts, under a cache of a few footprints, on CPU with NVMe, 512 experts. D2MoE has nested read-in-steps with a learned per-token bit-width router and SSD (GPU); HOBBIT has the importance-thresholded substitution at batch 1 with SSD on llama.cpp (GPU); DynaExq has hot/cold precision tiers in HBM. None combines nested residual bytes + top-rank rule + CPU + tiny cache; the published ablations (HOBBIT 1.19-1.57x, D2MoE +MWQ 1.9-4.95x vs multi-version, +Budget 1.06-1.21x) are in the same range as our replay (1.43-1.52x bytes, 1.17x serial, 1.00x overlap at 8 GB). No paper reports a quality number for a 512-expert top-10 model.
