Memory check (#10). Expert parallelism across machines is well known (DeepSpeed-MoE, Megatron, Tutel; for edge: EdgeMoE-style and the Prism/OD-MoE line). Closest published work: OD-MoE (distribute expert load and compute over cheap nodes, but GPU, tiny expert count, predictor-heavy) and Mesh LLM (practical llama.cpp expert sharding). Our variant differs where it matters: CPU nodes, each streaming from its own NVMe, 512 experts so the straggler is about 1.25, and the dense part stays serial.

Verdict (#10). Novelty: variant (the architecture is known; the disk-bound 512-expert two-laptop regime with measured straggler is not published). Published evidence supports the speedup in our regime: no. OD-MoE's 75%-of-cached figure and Prism's 30.6% are GPU, 1 Gbps with one expert per worker, or simulated; exo's 5.37 tok/s is all-in-RAM. Not applicable to us: every headline number above (GPU, 8-expert Mixtral or all-resident models, different baselines). Our measurement stands: about 1.7x at 8 GB serial from cache pooling, about 1.1x with overlap, needs two machines.

Memory check (#11). Remote-memory tiers for model weights are well known in datacenters (CXL pooling, RDMA far memory, vLLM/LMCache offload); the specific "10 layers on NVMe, 38 in a peer's RAM" has no direct publication that I know. Closest: RMAI (remote shared memory for experts, unverified beyond abstract) and Harvest (peer GPU memory as a cache tier). Our variant is in a regime where the local NVMe (3.1 GB/s) is already about as fast as a 25 GbE link, so the remote tier offers nothing.

Memory check (#12). Activation sparsity inside FFNs (Deja Vu, CATS, TEAL, PowerInfer, LLM in a flash, FloE) is well known, and intra-expert sparsity in MoEs was published at least twice before the screen's idea was framed. Closest work: FloE (offloaded experts, up-projection-driven mask) and PowerInfer-2 (neuron bundles read from flash, gate first). Our variant differs only in the regime (CPU, batch 1, SwiGLU experts read from NVMe, 512 experts so fewer repeated hits) and in block granularity, which is where the literature has real fragmentation evidence (PowerInfer-2 reports ~1 GB/s for 4 KB random reads vs ~4 GB/s sequential on UFS).

Verdict (#12). Novelty: known (the mechanism); open only for the NVMe-on-CPU block-I/O combination. Published evidence supports a speedup in our regime: no, not as measured: all speed numbers are GPU or phone, resident or PCIe; the one CPU-adjacent paper (PowerInfer-2) needs ReLU-trained models and is on a phone. Not applicable to us: TEAL 1.5-1.8x (A6000, FP16 dense), TurboSparse 2-5x (retrained), PowerInfer-2 27.8x (baseline llama.cpp with the model not fitting), FloE 48.7x (weak baseline). Our measurement (closed form 1.17-1.5x) agrees with the 2605.08575 ceiling and the TEAL scale; d is still unmeasured, and that single number decides it.

Our measurement: chunked prefill reads 8.3x (c=16) and 5.3x (c=32) more unique expert reads than one batch; at 7 tok/s typing no sub-batch chunk keeps up; TTFT speedup 0.17-0.84x for chunks below 128, 1.0x at best; only at 1-2 tok/s typing does 5-7x appear. Decode tok/s unchanged.

Memory check (#13). Prefix KV caching is standard (vLLM, SGLang, llama.cpp prompt cache, Anthropic/OpenAI prompt caching). "Prefill while the user types" is the idea behind LiveMind, PredGen, and in practice MAFDET, plus Google's patent for the assistant-command analogue. Closest work: MAFDET (local, llama.cpp, exact common-prefix prefill) and LiveMind. Our variant differs in a way that matters: the model is a streamed-expert MoE, where small chunks multiply unique expert reads (8.3x at 16 tokens) and typing is slower than the disk, which the dense-model papers never face.

Verdict (#13). Novelty: known (typing-time prefill) with an open corner (MoE with streamed experts, where it is mostly a loss). Published evidence supports a speedup in our regime: no. The dense-model results (MAFDET 12.2 to 3.2 s on a 4B, LiveMind 6.3x) are on small or GPU-resident models; LMCache's 13.6x is a server multi-turn prefix cache; SpecPrefill is a different technique. Our measurement (TTFT 0.17-0.84x for chunks below 128 tokens at 7 tok/s typing; helps only below 3.5 tok/s) is a new local result, but it uses decode traces as a proxy for prefill routing and 249-299 token traces. Row P-0205 should be updated (feature shipped in some form) and P-0207 re-checked or dropped.
