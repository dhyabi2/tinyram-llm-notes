Prior art we may have missed (family 2):
- QuIP# (Tseng et al., ICML 2024, arXiv 2402.04396; opened via search result text only, not the paper): E8P 8-dim lattice codebook, decoded in under 4 instructions per weight, over 50% of RTX 4090 bandwidth. This is the origin of IQ2 and shows the 8-dim 256-entry codebook is known. Threatens novelty of the codebook; says nothing for CPU LUT-accumulate.
- Pushing the Envelope of LLM Inference on AI-PC and Intel GPUs (Georganas, Kalamkar, Heinecke, arXiv 2508.06753v2, opened): 2-bit AVX2 GEMV within 10-20% of the int8 roofline using shuffle up-convert then int8 VNNI dot; argues FMA/dot beats LUT GEMMs; 2.2x over bitnet.cpp on one CPU. Strengthens our negative microbenchmark (0.06-0.17x).
- CodeGEMM (Park et al., NeurIPS 2025, opened abstract): "Psumbook" of centroid-activation inner products gathered by code index; 1.83x (8B), 8.93x (70B) over prior codebook methods at 2 bit; hardware not named on page (likely GPU). Closest to our LUT idea; no CPU evidence.
- QMoE (Frantar, Alistarh, arXiv 2310.16795, opened): sub-1-bit MoE compression, GPU decode kernels, under 5% overhead. VQ-like MoE compression is known since 2023.
- BitsMoE (arXiv 2606.00079v2, opened): shared-basis spectral split with per-unit bit allocation, 2-bit Qwen3-30B 64.29% avg vs 42.65% GPTQ; A6000 decode. Bridges families 2 and 5, GPU.
- MoQE (arXiv 2310.02410, search text only): 2-bit expert-only scalar quantisation, 79.6% size cut, 1.24x on A100.
- From memory, not opened: bitnet.cpp TL1/TL2 LUT kernels, QTIP, VPTQ, GPTVQ, Marlin, KTransformers' quantised CPU experts.

Memory check: low-bit LUT kernels (T-MAC, bitnet.cpp) and VQ quantisers (AQLM, QuIP#, VPTQ) are well known; shipped llama.cpp IQ2_* is already an 8-d 256-entry grid. What is not published (searches found no AVX2 kernel combining a vector codebook with LUT-accumulate) is that exact CPU kernel, but our own microbenchmark says it loses by 5-16x on AVX2 gather, and Intel's paper points the same way.

Verdict #2: novelty = known (codebook, IQ2) with the LUT-accumulate CPU kernel as an open-but-negative variant. Published evidence for a speedup in our regime: no; every headline is against dequant or FP32 baselines, GPUs, or Arm. Our measurement: bytes half 1.57x serial / 1.09x overlap at 8 GB, 1.0x at 16/32 GB; compute half refuted (0.06-0.17x of int8 dot).
Numbers that cannot apply: T-MAC 6.6x/3.6x and AQLM 2.75-4x (dequant/FP32 baseline), KBVQ 1.6x (GPU), CARDAN (Trainium).

New prior art (family 3):
- Prompt Cache (Gim et al., MLSys 2024, arXiv 2311.04934, opened): modular attention reuse, TTFT 8x GPU, 60x CPU. Same lever; CPU result is the closest regime.
- CacheBlend (arXiv 2405.16444, search text) and A3 (arXiv 2511.17560, search text): non-prefix and on-disk precomputed KV; not opened in full.

From memory: vLLM automatic prefix caching, SGLang RadixAttention/HiCache, Mooncake, Anthropic/Gemini context caching.
All show the feature is known and shipping; none changes decode tok/s.

Memory check: prefix KV reuse is thoroughly known (2023 onward). Our variant (per-model/quant/router key for a disk-streamed 125B where cold prefill costs a 69 GB stream) differs only in magnitude of benefit: cold TTFT 22-42 s vs 0.4 s on hit.

Verdict #3: novelty = known (ships in stock llama.cpp). Published evidence supports a TTFT gain (8-60x, hearth 55x) but not a decode speedup; for decode the answer is no. Our measurement: decode exactly 1.0x; TTFT hit ratio 48-51x; 200-token request 1.24x; OLMoE plumbing run byte-identical output, 131,072 B/token. Cannot apply: Tutti, py-kvcache, SGLang-LSM numbers (GPU servers, request rates, hit-rate gains over another cache).

New prior art (family 4):
- Fate (arXiv 2502.12224, opened abstract): cross-layer gate for prefetch, 97% accuracy claim (search text), decode up to 4.1x vs load-on-demand; hardware not on page. Directly the same idea, earlier than the screen. Threatens novelty.
- FlashMoE (arXiv 2601.17063v2, opened): SSD experts, ML cache replacement, hit +51% vs LRU/LFU, up to 2.6x; desktop, models not named. Closest regime (SSD, desktop).
- Edge0, "The Other Half of the Memory Wall" (arXiv 2609.18063, opened): 35B MoE from SSD at 20 tok/s with 3 GiB, trained prerouter prediction one token ahead plus LoRA recovery; 24 GB machine. A trained predictor works where id co-occurrence does not, but needs training and tolerates quality loss.
- Flash-MoE write-up (opened, Mar 2026): Qwen3.5-397B, M3 Max 48 GB, 4.36 tok/s, 17.5 GB/s SSD, 71% OS page cache hit; prefetch cut speed about 73% on Apple Silicon. Strongly supports our result in a close regime (single blog source).
- Who Should Own the Expert Cache? (arXiv 2608.12103, opened): router lookahead as readahead changed median time 0.3%; perfect one-layer advice only 5.0%; static frequency pinning 1.09-1.11x. Matches our oracle-bound analysis.
- SpecMD (arXiv 2602.03921, opened): expert access does not follow LRU/LFU locality; Least-Stale eviction, 88% hit at 5% VRAM; OLMoE.
- DALI (arXiv 2602.03495, opened): RTX 3090 + 256 GB DDR4, residual-based prefetch, 3.97x decode vs llama.cpp avg (GPU-CPU split). Cache-conditional experts (arXiv 2412.00099, TMLR 2025, opened): cache-aware routing, 2x on mobile (lossy routing change). MoE-Infinity (arXiv 2401.14361, search text). SSD offload energy study (arXiv 2508.06978, opened; simulation, GPUs). AdapMoE, SeqMoE, PreScope (search text only). From memory: Pre-gated MoE (ISCA 2024), Mixtral-offloading (Eliseev), Fiddler, Apple "LLM in a Flash" (2023), PowerInfer, kTransformers.

Memory check: expert prefetch from a gate/hidden-state prediction is a crowded, well-known area (2023-2026). Closest to our regime: HOBBIT (llama.cpp, NVMe, batch 1), Flash-MoE and Budgeting Bytes (storage-bound, prefetch fails). Our variant differs by regime (disk-bound CPU, 2-8 footprint cache, top-10 of 512), and in that regime published and local evidence converge on prefetch gaining little.

Verdict #4: novelty = known. Evidence for a speedup in our regime: no (ProMoE and Fate are PCIe/DRAM; Budgeting Bytes, Flash-MoE, 2608.12103 all report none or negative). Our measurement: 1.00-1.075x at 2.1 footprints with held-out id predictor (precision 0.31-0.43); oracle top-4/top-8 with one-layer lead 1.25x/1.59x. Open point: hidden-state predictors (91-98% accuracy) were not testable on id traces; even then the one-layer lead and shared disk bound gains (paper 2608.12103: 5%). Cannot apply: ProMoE 2.07x/5.02x, HOBBIT 9.93x, Fate 4.1x, DALI 3.97x, Edge0 20 tok/s (all GPU / big-DRAM / lossy / trained).

New prior art (family 5):
- MC-SMoE "Merge, Then Compress" (Li et al., ICLR 2024, arXiv 2310.01334, opened): merge then low-rank, up to 80% memory, 20% FLOPs. Earliest of the shared-structure family.
- Sub-MoE (arXiv 2506.23266, opened): joint SVD, shared U per group; 96%/86% retained at 25%/50% expert reduction on Mixtral.
- CARDAN (arXiv 2609.21137, opened abstract): VQ + shared-basis low-rank per expert matrix, 1.15-1.31x on Trainium, ppl matches BF16 teacher. Nearest to our family as proposed (resident basis + quantised per-expert part); accelerator only; strengthens that the idea is published but with modest gain.
- BitsMoE (arXiv 2606.00079v2, opened): shared basis + expert-specific spectral directions with ILP bit allocation, 2-bit.
- RFID-MoE (arXiv 2602.09316), LorExperts (listed Aug 2026), MoLAE (cited in MoBE): search text only. LorExperts' authors reportedly say a single shared component degrades as experts grow (unverified).
- From memory: MoE-I2, EAC-MoE, Delta-CoMe, BitDelta (delta compression for fine-tuned models), QMoE.

Memory check: shared base + per-expert delta is well known (MC-SMoE 2023 onward, D2-MoE, MoBE, MoE-SVD). All publish 24-60% parameter cuts with measurable quality loss and GPU speedups, none for NVMe streaming. Our variant (resident basis, 2-3 bit residual streamed) differs in intent but the closest published outcomes (1.3-2.3x byte cut) match our closed-form 1.5-1.75x.

Verdict #5: novelty = known (variant on streamed residual). Evidence for a speedup in our regime: no. Our measurement: OLMoE layer-8 probe finds no shared low-rank structure (16 of 64 components = 27.8% of centred energy vs 25% chance); 3.6x would need 1.19 bpw; at most 1.53x serial / 1.09x overlap at 8 GB even with a free basis; rank-16 resident basis = 2.07 GB. MoBE's own argument (effective rank above SVD thresholds) agrees. Cannot apply: D2-MoE and MoE-SVD speeds (batch 64 GPU), DeltaMoE 2.57x (merged models), 2512.17073 5-8x (FP16 PCIe baseline).
