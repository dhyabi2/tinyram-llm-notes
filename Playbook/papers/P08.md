Prior art we may have missed (opened = fetched):
- Router-Tuning, He et al., EMNLP 2025, arXiv 2410.13184 (opened abstract): router-only fine-tuning for dynamic depth, 21% speedup at 0.2% drop. Strengthens: realistic gain scale ~20%. Does not threaten the MoE-streaming variant.
- Not All Experts are Equal, Lu et al., ACL 2024, arXiv 2402.14800 (opened abstract; 1.2x Mixtral from search snippet): expert-level dynamic skipping in Mixtral. Neighbouring idea (skip experts not layers).
- MoDES (CVPR 2026, arXiv 2511.15690, search snippet only): dynamic expert skipping, 1.24x decode on Qwen3-VL-MoE-30B-A3B. Weakly relevant.
- From memory: Mixture-of-Depths (Raposo et al. 2024), LayerDrop (Fan et al. 2020), CALM (Schuster et al. 2022), "The Unreasonable Ineffectiveness of the Deeper Layers" (Gromov et al. 2024), FlexiDepth (named inside Dr.LLM's Table 5). All dense-model.
- Search found no work using adjacent-layer router similarity for layer skipping in MoE (search snippet statement, not proof).

Memory check: layer skipping/early exit is thoroughly known. Nothing I know targets skipping to save streamed expert bytes in a disk-bound MoE; that regime is the only difference, and the byte saving is plain 1/kept.

Verdict #6: novelty known (the layer skip) with the streamed-MoE framing a thin variant; published evidence for a speedup in our regime: no (no paper measures it; the published gains are 10-26% on dense GPU models and quality cost is real). Our measurement: arithmetic 1/kept (3.03x at 67% skip at 16-32 GB overlap), adjacent-layer expert Jaccard 0.031-0.033 equals chance (0.034), quality unmeasured. Headline numbers that cannot apply: all of them (dense, GPU, 10-25% skip).

## Family #7: session-pinned RAM set from prefill

| id | paper | setting | headline numbers | catalog summary | transfers? | strongest caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P-0172 | MoE-Infinity, Xue, Fu, Lu, Mai, Marina; arXiv 2401.14361 v3, 12 Mar 2025 | RTX A5000 24GB, PCIe 4.0, weights in host RAM 32 GB-1 TB; Switch, DeepSeek-V2-Lite, NLLB, Mixtral, Arctic; batch 1, prompt 512, decode 32 | abstract 3.1-16.7x (conclusion 2.7-13.7x); Table 1 DeepSeek-V2-Lite TPOT 155 ms vs vLLM 485, DeepSpeed 737, Mixtral-Offloading 1250, Ollama 2590; Mixtral worst case ~836 ms, 1.4x over Mixtral-Offloading | accurate | no: GPU + host RAM, not NVMe-to-CPU | method is request-level activation-matrix matching with cosine similarity plus prefetch and layer-discounted eviction, not a static frequency pin; wins are against weak baselines |

New prior art (opened = fetched):
- FlashMoE, arXiv 2601.17063 (opened HTML): SSD-offloaded MoE on a desktop (RTX 5070 Ti, 64 GB DDR5, NVMe 7.4 GB/s), OLMoE and Qwen3-30B-A3B, ML cache policy trained on Belady labels; LRU 73% vs Belady 86% hit rate; up to 2.6x decode vs existing systems, but only ~7% faster than LRU on Qwen3-30B-A3B. Strengthens: in our regime the best-known gain over LRU on a larger model is small. Partial threat to novelty of any cache policy (SSD regime is covered).
- SpecMD, ICML 2026 (opened abstract): finds MoE access does not follow LRU/LFU temporal-locality assumptions; Least-Stale policy cuts collision misses up to 85x vs LRU on OLMoE. Mixed: contradicts a flat "LRU is enough" and our result is only on 3 traces.
- Fewer Expert Transfers Without Changing Routing, NeurIPS 2026 workshop poster (opened): eviction-only change cuts transfers 2.1x; a past-only frequency rule recovers 27% of the gain, future demand over 16 tokens recovers 96%. Supports our finding that frequency pinning alone is weak.
- Paging the Experts, arXiv 2609.29032 (opened): flash-backed MoE on iPhone (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B); 512 MiB LRU gives 0.00% hits vs 18.8% for random eviction; 576 MiB LRU 38.58%. Shows a capacity cliff for LRU at tiny caches, relevant to our 2-8 footprint regime.
- SeqMoE (arXiv 2609.12978, abstract opened): 96.97% hit rate at 45% residency; also named in our pubtech notes as unmeasured.
- HOBBIT (arXiv 2411.01433, opened): llama.cpp-based, RTX 4090 and Jetson, batch 1; also relevant to #9.
- llama.cpp issue 20757 (search snippet only, not opened): feature request for a protected hot-expert segment in the eviction policy; pinning idea already proposed.
- From memory: Pre-gated MoE, ProMoE, AdapMoE, Fiddler, Mixtral-offloading (LRU + speculative prefetch).

Memory check: activation-frequency-guided caching is well known (MoE-Infinity, Mixtral-offloading's LRU, FlashMoE's recency+frequency). Pinning a set from prefill is a small variant.

Verdict #7: novelty known/variant; published support for a gain over a warmed LRU in our regime: no (past-only frequency is weak in the 2026 eviction paper; FlashMoE gains over LRU 7-22%). Our measurement agrees: mean pin minus LRU +0.014 over 3 traces, LRU wins by 0.05-0.09 over a longer horizon. Headline numbers not applicable: MoE-Infinity's 3.1-16.7x (GPU, weak baselines, batch 1 but host RAM).

New prior art:
- AQLM, Egiazarian et al., arXiv 2401.06118 (search results only, not opened): multi-codebook additive quantization with CPU lookup-table dot products, up to 4x over FP32 on an 8-core i9 (per search summary); CPU kernel for 8-bit codebooks. Threatens novelty of "LUT-based VQ dot products on CPU" for dense models.
- TriS-MoE, NeurIPS 2026 (opened abstract): sub-2-bit MoE, metadata raises effective bits to ~3; 6.32x memory reduction, no latency. Strengthens our concern that codebook/metadata overhead eats the nominal bpw.
- HOBBIT, FlashMoE (see above): same bytes lever on offloaded MoE.
- From memory: QuIP#, QTIP, MoEQuant, EAQuant, MoE-Quantization-Bench, Any-Precision LLM.

Memory check: VQ for LLMs (AQLM, GPTVQ, VPTQ, QuIP#) and for MoE (KBVQ-MoE, QMoE) are well known. Plain 8-dim k-means at 1 bpw is the weakest variant; no paper claims it works.

Verdict #8: novelty known; published support at 1.3 bpw: no (published VQ reaches near-FP16 only at ~3 bits; KBVQ at 2.08 bits loses ~5 accuracy points). Our measurement agrees: 1.0 bpw error 0.58 vs 0.33 for a Q2_K-like scalar, gain capped 2.0x serial / 1.09x overlap at 8 GB, ~1.0x overlap at 16-32 GB. Headline numbers not applicable: QMoE (GPU, Switch), KBVQ 1.58x (GPU BF16 baseline), MC speedups (GPU custom kernel).

## Family #9: 2-bit base plus 2-bit residual for the top-4 experts

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P-0182 | D2MoE, Wang, Zhou, Hong, Guo, MobiCom '25, arXiv 2504.15299 | LLaMA-MoE-3.5B, Mixtral-8x7B; RTX 3060 6GB + 32 GB RAM + 970 EVO (3.5 GB/s) and Jetson AGX Orin; concurrency 4-32 | up to 1.39x throughput, up to 53% less peak memory; PPL Mixtral 4.09 vs 4.04 INT8; dequant overhead 5-20% | accurate | partly | ladders INT2->4 or INT5->8; per-token bit router needs 2-4 h fine-tune; GPU-assisted; gain over best baseline 1.14-1.39x |

| P-0184 | URL is GEMQ (Deng et al., arXiv 2605.23078, 21 May 2026); DynaExq is arXiv 2511.15015 (Chu et al.) | GEMQ: DeepSeekV2-Lite, Qwen1.5-MoE, Mixtral; H100. New prior art (this is where novelty is most threatened):
- SliceMoE, Choi et al., DAC 2026, arXiv 2512.12990 (opened abstract): bit-sliced expert caching, caches experts at slice granularity with on-demand precision, matryoshka-style truncation quantization (AMAT), cache warmup during prefill; DeepSeek-V2-Lite and Qwen1.5-MoE; decode latency up to 1.81x/1.64x, energy 2.37x/2.85x. Closest published match to family #9; also touches #7 (prefill warmup).
- HOBBIT, arXiv 2411.01433 (opened): on a cache miss load important experts at high precision and the rest low (67% high / 30% low / 3% skipped on Mixtral); llama.cpp-based, batch 1, up to 9.93x vs offloading baselines, accuracy loss within 1% on GSM8K/TruthfulQA. Uses gate score as the importance signal, like our top-4 rule.
- DynaExq, arXiv 2511.15015 (opened abstract): hotness-driven runtime precision per expert under a hard memory budget.
- DyMoE, arXiv 2603.19172 (opened abstract): mixed-precision expert cache with prefetching, TTFT 3.44-22.7x, TPOT up to 14.58x vs offloading, hardware unstated.
- From memory: Any-Precision LLM (Park et al. 2024), EdgeMoE (Yi et al. 2023, per-expert bitwidth + SSD loading, named as a D2MoE baseline).

Memory check: nested/bit-sliced precision for experts plus rank- or hotness-driven selection is already published several times (HOBBIT, D2MoE, SliceMoE, DynaExq). Our variant (tiny cache of 2-8 footprints, CPU, NVMe, base resident, enhancement loaded for the top-4 only) differs in regime, not in mechanism.

Verdict #9: novelty variant, leaning known; published evidence in our regime: unclear to no (all GPU; best SSD-related result, D2MoE, gains 1.14-1.39x; SliceMoE 1.64-1.81x at unknown hardware). Our measurement: bytes ratio 1.43-1.52x at 2.2 footprints, 1.47x at the real 8 GB footprint, 1.17x serial and 1.00x overlap at 8 GB, compute-bound above; post-hoc nesting is worse than a 4-bit scalar (error 0.085 vs 0.040). Headline numbers that cannot apply: D2MoE 53% memory and 1.39x (GPU-assisted, concurrency), GEMQ 82.5 tok/s and DynaExq 2.73x (H100/HBM, batch 32), HOBBIT 9.93x (against weak baselines, GPU).
