First pass JSON: "decode 0.288->1.992 (6.92x), 0.282->1.846 (6.55x); Kimi 3.30-3.52x; hit 17-54%": all correct (hit range is 17.0-54.2%). - Ablation: none in the blog (the blog says the gain is the combined effect of layout, direct I/O, pinned staging, GPU cache). It does not say whether the VRAM cache was on in that ablation.

This blog (6.5-6.9x vs Ollama, 3.3-3.5x vs llama.cpp --cpu-moe, though with a GPU cache in the SGLang arm) and the other rows here (Flash-MoE "mmap 5x slower", BigMoeOnEdge ~25x) indicate that stock mmap with working set far above RAM is much slower than our emulation suggests. See "Beliefs" below.

## P-0094 BigMoeOnEdge, gpt-oss-120b on an 11.3 GB phone

- Title: "gpt-oss-120b - a 58 GB model at 5.2x device RAM" (docs/benchmarks-gpt-oss.md, repo Helldez/BigMoeOnEdge, raw file). Authors not named in the file. Measurements dated 2026-07-17 on main @ 1419af3; older sweep 2026-07-14 (bmoe-cli @ 4d12b75, 24-token probes, best 0.687 tok/s).
- Setup: 12 GB Android phone (Android 16 per the page), 11.3 GB usable RAM, arm64 NDK r29; gpt-oss-120b-Q4_K_M.gguf 58.46 GB, 36 layers, 128 experts, top-4 default, MXFP4 expert weights; data on real /data partition (O_DIRECT works; /sdcard FUSE silently falls back). Flash bandwidth not stated. Batch 1, 256 tokens, one fixed prompt, greedy, `-t 4 --no-think --moe-stream --overlap --dense-weights anon`.
- Table (256-token steady state), tok/s: k=2, cache 2000 MiB, 8 lanes 2.191 (flash read 590.3 MiB/token, hit 32.0%, CPU throttled at 1.55 GHz); k=2 cache off 4 lanes 1.790 (908.5 MiB); k=4 cache 2000 MiB 8 lanes 1.300 (1292.4 MiB, hit 27.1%); k=4 cache 2000 4 lanes 0.998; k=4 cache off 4 lanes 0.711 (1817.0 MiB). The lane-4 vs lane-8 pair is confounded by thermal state (the page says so).
- Dense-weights policy table: majflt/token 314-1894 (page-cached "warm") vs 6-10 (O_DIRECT anon); compute 0.948 -> 0.156 s/tok.
- mmap baseline (24-token probes, older build): 11.24 s/token (k=2), 13.34 s/token (k=4). That is 0.089 and 0.075 tok/s. My ratio 2.191/0.089 = 24.6x (page "~25x", labelled indicative, not matched A/B).
- Ratios I computed: headline 2.191/0.687 = 3.19x (page 3.2x) but this mixes k=4 (0.687, old sweep, dense page-cached, 24 tokens, auto cache up to 3000 MB) with k=2 (2.191, new protocol). Like-for-like at k=4: 1.300/0.687 = 1.89x (still mixed protocols). Effect of k=2 vs k=4 at same config: 2.191/1.300 = 1.69x (page +68.5%). Effect of cache at k=4, lane 4: 0.998/0.711 = 1.40x. k=4 cache off at new protocol 0.711 vs old sweep 0.687: 1.03x.
- Catalog row: "Routing weights through O_DIRECT instead of the page cache raised throughput 0.687 to 2.191 tok/s (3.2x)": numbers right, mechanism WRONG. It is the dense (non-expert) weights (embeddings, attention, norms, lm_head) read via O_DIRECT into anonymous buffers (`--dense-weights anon`); experts were already streamed via O_DIRECT. Also the 2.191 includes the switch to top-k 2 (a lossy change) and protocol change. First-pass JSON: "best 2.191 (top-k 2); top-k 4 1.300; cache off 0.711; mmap 11-13 s/token": correct; "summary_accuracy wrong": correct; its note on O_DIRECT is correct.
- Conditions: ARM phone, 4 threads, cpu-only (no GPU mention), model 5.2x RAM, 2000 MiB expert cache, tight coupling to RAM pressure on non-expert weights.
- Transfers: partly. Regime match (CPU, tiny RAM vs model, batch 1, NVMe-class flash, mixed MoE) is the closest of the batch, and the finding "non-expert weights in the page cache get evicted and cause a fault storm that shows up as compute; fix by owning them in anonymous memory" is a mechanism we may hit at 8 GB. Not transferable: the headline 3.2x (k-reduction and protocol change inside it), phone flash speed, 128 experts top-4 vs our 512 top-10.
- Quality loss: only `--no-think` mode and a one-prompt check: "17 x 23": k=2 391 (right), k=3 391, k=4 (default) 387 (wrong); capital of Australia right at all k; "both k=2 and k=4 stayed coherent" on one essay prompt. No benchmark. The page admits no-think is a latency mode.
- Ablation: yes for the relevant part: dense policy (314-1894 vs 6-10 majflt/token, compute 0.948 -> 0.156 s/token), cache on vs off (0.998 vs 0.711 at k=4 lane 4), k=2 vs 4, prefetch=4 regresses (reads 20-25% more speculatively, only 12-15% used; from the provenance paragraph, older sweep).
- Limitations stated: not all cells from the same device state (thermal throttling), lane comparison confounded, mmap rows from an older build and 24-token probes, single prompt.
- Contradicts us? It says a bounded expert cache lost to no cache when dense weights shared the page cache (older result), and wins after dense moved out of it. Our simulations treat cache size as independent of what else competes for RAM. Not a contradiction, a missing term (see Beliefs).

## P-0095 Flash-MoE (urandom.io post, README and paper of danveloper/flash-moe)

- Sources read: urandom.io post "Zero Python, Zero Frameworks, 397 Billion Parameters: Flash-MoE Is Kind of Absurd" by "Bender" (an agent byline), Mar 22, 2026 (secondary); the repo README.md and paper/flash_moe.pdf (702-line layout text; repo last pushed 2026-03-19; paper authors not named in the text I extracted, the README says built by a human and an AI in 24 hours); results.tsv listed (58 experiments per README) but only the README/paper tables were used. Lilting page: UNREAD (not fetched).
- Setup: MacBook Pro M3 Max, 48 GB unified (about 400 GB/s), 1 TB Apple SSD with 17.5 GB/s measured sequential read; Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, 60 layers (45 GatedDeltaNet + 15 full attention), 512 experts per layer, default K = 10 (paper Sec. 4.5 and Table 9; the post and README say "K=4 activate", which is the reduced setting), one shared expert; 4-bit experts 209 GB (7.08 MB each), 2-bit 120 GB (3.93 MB). Non-expert weights 5.5 GB mmap'd. Software: C/Objective-C/Metal, parallel pread() (4 pthreads, GCD), OS page cache, no custom cache. Batch 1.
- Numbers: README: 4-bit FMA kernel 4.36 tok/s (4.4 headline), 4-bit baseline 3.90, 2-bit "trust OS" 5.74, 2-bit peak single token 7.05; 2-bit "breaks JSON/tool calling" (`\name\` instead of `"name"`). Paper Table 1 (avg tok/s): MLX baseline mmap 1.03; MLX packed + K=4 3.14; C/Metal GPU attention 4-bit cache 0: 5.29; +LRU 500 entries (3.5 GB) 4.80; 2-bit 5.55; +F_NOCACHE 5.68; Trust OS 5.74. Paper Table 3 (K sweep, tok/s, I/O MB/token): K=10 1.20 (2,359), K=6 1.91 (1,415), K=4 3.15 (943), K=3 and K=2 collapse (EOS only). My ratio K=4 vs K=10 = 3.15/1.20 = 2.63x (paper "2.6x"). Page cache analysis (Sec. 4.6): temporal overlap 8-34% per layer at K=4, LRU hit rates 49/59/68/71% for 16/32/64/128 entries per layer, cross-layer correlation near zero. Layer timing: 4.28 ms avg (README, 4-bit), 2.9 ms (2-bit, paper), I/O 1.37 ms (47%) per layer. I/O floor at 17.5 GB/s: 53.9 ms/token for 943 MB (18.6 tok/s).
- Catalog numbers: "pread streaming of experts from SSD, about 4.4 tok/s": correct for K=4 4-bit (README 4.36). "non-expert weights about 5.5 GB resident": correct. Not in the one-liner but first-pass facts: 5.74 2-bit: correct; "~35GB OS page cache (71% hit)": the README says page cache ~35 GB and ~71% hit; the paper's 71% is the 128-entry-per-layer LRU simulation (128 of 512 experts = 25% of the pool), not a measurement of the OS cache: the two statements are not the same quantity; treat 71% as unverified for the OS cache. First-pass note "whether K=4 is reduced is unverified": RESOLVED, it is reduced from the model default K=10 (paper Table 9, Sec. 4.5), so 4.4 tok/s is a lossy configuration. First-pass "custom caches slower": correct (Table 6: Metal LRU+F_NOCACHE 4.11, Metal LRU no F_NOCACHE 4.80, OS only 5.74; LRU 3.5 GB 4.80 vs 5.29 without).
- Internal inconsistencies I found: Table 1 lists 4-bit rows between 2.83 and 5.29 tok/s, README says 4.36 best 4-bit; 4.4 appears only in README/post, not in the paper table; the paper says 2-bit quality is "indistinguishable from 4-bit" and "Excellent" while the README says 2-bit breaks JSON/tool calling. The paper is a hastily written artefact (author states AI-assisted).
- Conditions: M3 Max unified memory (SSD DMA and GPU compete for the same memory controller; they found overlap of I/O and GPU compute counter-productive and F_RDADVISE prefetch net 0%), 17.5 GB/s SSD, K=4 not 10, 35-40 GB of page cache for a 209 GB pool (17-19% of experts).
- Transfers: partly (as first pass). What carries: pread of one contiguous expert blob per read; OS page cache beating a custom cache when RAM is scarce; mmap on cold expert reads about 5x slower (Table 4). Does not carry: 17.5 GB/s SSD vs our about 3 GB/s (5.8x; at 3 GB/s the paper's K=4 I/O floor 943 MB would be 0.31 s/token = 3.2 tok/s at best, while our top-10 needs about 1.3 GB/token = 0.43 s/token), GPU compute, reduced K (our model is top-10 and we do not want to cut it), Apple unified memory effects.
- Quality: K 10 -> 4 "no observable quality loss" judged over 200-token generations on 5 prompts (Table 3 "Excellent"), K=3 collapses; 2-bit requantization RMSE only (Table 2), 3 qualitative prompts. No benchmark scores.
- Ablation: yes, extensive negative-results table (58 experiments): LZ4 -13%, F_RDADVISE prefetch net 0%, temporal prediction -18% (25% hit), MLP predictor 31% accuracy, expert file clustering 0% ("NVMe ignores scatter at 7MB granularity"), mmap -5x, dispatch_io -70%, MTP speculative decoding break-even, speculative early routing -38%. These are on Apple hardware with K=4.
- Contradicts our findings? Their cross-layer correlation "near zero" and MLP predictor 31% accuracy conflicts in direction with the family that depends on layer-to-layer prediction; our D.md used layer-to-layer co-occurrence on Qwen3-30B (128 experts); different model and K, not a direct contradiction but a warning. Their "expert file clustering 0%" matches our result that clustering adds under 1.04x (C.md).

## P-0096 Jetson Orin Nano streaming fork (dev.to)

- Title: "Running a 26B MoE on an 8 GB Jetson by streaming experts from SSD", Anish Shrestha (dev.to user anyesh), published 2026-08-01 (dev.to API id 4287871). Design: dense weights resident, per-layer pool of N expert slots (LFU), misses pread from the GGUF by a thread pool, slot-id remapping so no CUDA kernel changes, overlap by reordering the graph so the shared dense FFN computes while reads run. Batch 1. Pool in pinned host memory, shared with GPU.
- Numbers: `--cpu-moe` mmap baseline 1.94 tok/s; streaming 32 slots x 8 IO threads, overlap on 2.95 (my ratio 1.52x); overlap off 2.23 (1.15x vs baseline; overlap worth 2.95/2.23 = 1.32x, page says 32%). Live request 2.09 tok/s. NVMe random-read ceiling about 1.27 GB/s at this block size (fio) so IO-bound limit near 5 tok/s (implies about 0.25 GB per token). 32 slots = 3 GB pinned pool; 64 slots = 6 GB starves the system; peak RSS 6.7 GB; 32 greedy steps bit-identical on the 26B, 10.5 GiB streamed. Cold prefill about 1/4 of baseline speed. Verified bitwise identity on three families.
- Catalog row numbers (8 IO threads, 2.95 vs 1.94, 1.5x): correct. First-pass JSON: all correct (2.95/2.23/1.94, IO-bound ~5, 32-slot LFU, cold prefill 1/4). First-pass "transfers: yes": I downgrade to partly: unified-memory GPU box, model 13.3 GiB on 8 GB (1.7x RAM, we are 3-9x), baseline is the mmap `--cpu-moe` path, gain relies on overlapping reads with the shared-FFN compute on the GPU, ratio of cache slots to experts per layer unknown because expert count is not stated.
- Quality loss: none by construction (bitwise identical logits over 32 steps).
- Ablation: overlap on/off (2.95 vs 2.23); slot count 32 vs 64 (memory cliff, no tok/s given); no cache-vs-layout split (the baseline arm has no streaming and no cache).
- Limitations stated: cold prefill slower (no pre-faulting), one machine, O_DIRECT reads not yet done, prefill through pool is future work. Self-reported, single author.
- Contradicts us? No. The "IO-bound limit 5 tok/s vs 2.95 measured" suggests 59% of the IO ceiling reached, similar to Flash-MoE's 60% I/O efficiency (paper Sec. 5.1).

- Contradicts us: no. It supports that contiguous reads >= 32 KiB with parallel threads reach the flash throughput; our measured 0.9-4 MiB pread rates (2.4-2.8 GB/s) sit well above that regime already.

## P-0100 Edge0, arXiv 2609.18063v2

- Title: "The Other Half of the Memory Wall: Serving 35B MoEs from SSD with Trained Routing Prediction". Authors: Yu Lin, Yiming Wang, Runyuan Cai, Hanze Liu, Xiaodong Zeng (AutoArk). arXiv v2, 17 Sep 2026 (the page says "September 2026"). MLX backend only.
- Setup: edge0-35b = Qwen3.6-35B-A3B base, 40 layers x 256 experts + shared, base K=8 but released at K=4, int4 affine g64, checkpoint 19.5 GB (18.4 GiB with adapters), active about 3B; edge0-8b = Ling 3.0 tiny (MLA+MoE), 24 layers x 128 experts, K=8, 4.5 GB. Hardware: Mac mini M4 Pro 24 GB for Table 1 profiles; MacBook M2 16 GB for the prerouter A/B (Fig. 3, Table 3, 4); quality on a compute server with OpenCompass. SSD bandwidth not stated. ### What is actually new in OUR variant
Everything in these rows is GPU-centred or Apple-unified-memory-centred, or dense-model, or anecdotal. The pieces that exist: contiguous per-expert layout + direct I/O (SSD-LLaMA/SGLang, 2.6-4.1x over mmap GGUF on a GPU box), pread streaming with OS page cache on 17.5 GB/s SSD (Flash-MoE, with K reduced from 10 to 4), dense-weights-out-of-page-cache (BigMoeOnEdge), large chunks across threads (Apple 2023, dense). What the papers do not provide: a CPU-only measurement of the repack/pread gain over stock llama.cpp mmap with a model 2-9x larger than RAM on a roughly 3 GB/s NVMe, at top-10 of 512 experts, with no K reduction and no retraining. Our pubtech result (B.md: explicit 4 MiB vs mmap emulation 1.5-2.4x measured, 4x/7x arithmetic at 8 GB; 1.0x vs our own explicit-read path) is therefore not contradicted but also not matched by a published CPU-only datapoint. Closest papers: SSD-LLaMA (layout/direct I/O), Flash-MoE (CPU-side pread plus OS cache, Apple), BigMoeOnEdge (phone CPU, mmap vs streaming, dense-weights effect), Edge0 (K=4 + trained prediction). Novelty verdict per the data: the layout idea is known (variant); the CPU-only 125B/512-expert regime at 8-32 GB is not covered by any of them.

### Beliefs this changes about our own measurements
1. Stock-mmap multiplier: B.md says the 0.34 GB/s mmap figure was not reproduced (multi-thread memmove emulation was 3-6x faster) and the true multiplier over stock llama.cpp is unmeasured. Published data points for stock-style mmap vs explicit reads when the model far exceeds RAM: Flash-MoE "mmap expert files -5x" (Table 4), BigMoeOnEdge mmap 11-13 s/token vs 0.456 s/token (about 25x, not matched), SGLang 6.5-6.9x (Ollama, with a GPU cache in the SGLang arm) and 3.3-3.5x (llama.cpp --cpu-moe), SSD-LLaMA ablation 2.6-4.1x. All are with GPU or phone/Apple; none is CPU-only Linux/Mac x86. This suggests our emulation (1.5-2.4x) understates the gap to real stock mmap; we should run actual stock llama.cpp with mmap on the 18.6 GB Qwen3-30B file at a restricted RAM and measure tok/s. Do not take the 6.9x or 25x as our expected gain.
2. Cache competes with dense weights and the OS page cache: BigMoeOnEdge (cache lost to no cache until dense weights were moved to anonymous memory) and Flash-MoE (custom LRU 3.5 GB 4.80 vs 5.29 tok/s without it; OS-only 5.74) both show a fixed expert cache can lose when RAM is scarce. Our simulations (A-F.md) treat cache capacity (2.07/7.58/20.84 footprints at 8/16/32 GB) as independent of the non-expert working set and OS cache. At 8 GB this term may matter; it is unmodelled rather than refuted.
3. Edge0 3.9 tok/s is not an on-demand streaming baseline; the P-0100 catalog line, and the B.md line "supports needing a cache/prefetch", should not be used as evidence that naive streaming fails. In the paper's own Table 1 caption, streaming without the prerouter does 19.9 tok/s on a 24 GB M4 Pro. On a 16 GB machine where the weights do not fit, the prerouter adds +80-84% (Table 3), a smaller and different statement.
4. Headline speedups here are inflated by lossy changes: Flash-MoE 4.4 tok/s is K=4 vs default K=10 (2.63x of its speed comes from that, Table 3); BigMoeOnEdge 2.191 is k=2 vs default 4 (1.69x of the 3.2x); Edge0 20.4 is K=4 vs base K=8. Our target keeps top-10, so quality-preserving gains are the smaller numbers: Flash-MoE K=10 1.20 tok/s on 17.5 GB/s SSD, BigMoeOnEdge k=4 1.300 tok/s.
5. Catalog and B.md should replace: P-0094 mechanism (dense weights, not "routing weights"), P-0100 baseline (resident, not on-demand), P-0101 (no MoE statement), P-0097 (anecdote, 2x not tied to Kimi or any hardware).
