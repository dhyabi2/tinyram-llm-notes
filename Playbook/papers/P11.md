## Family #18: expert store in a peer machine's RAM over RDMA (idea P-0232)

Our measurement (F.md): at 8 GB the peer path (11 GB/s) gives 1.49x serial and 1.00x overlap over a local NVMe path (3.11 GB/s), about 1.0x at 16/32 GB, about 7x only against plain mmap. A 10GbE peer (1.1 GB/s) is slower than local NVMe. Closed form only; no real network run.

New prior art (searched 5 queries, opened 4):
- MLX issue 3208, "RDMA weight streaming" (upd.dev/ml-explore/mlx/issues/3208). Five M3 Ultra Mac Studios on a Thunderbolt 5 mesh; measured 3.7 to 5.2 GB/s broadcast; phase 4 proposes on-demand streaming of cold experts at about 50 ms per expert block at 5 GB/s (stated as speculative). Closed, no maintainer reply. It is the nearest thing to our idea and it measured 5 GB/s, not 11. It weakens novelty slightly (idea stated publicly) and weakens the speed premise (real TB5 RDMA is 3.7-5.2 GB/s in this report).
- RMAI, "Rethinking memory for AI (inference)", Noohi, Derispour, Barbalace (Edinburgh), EuroMLSys '25. In-kernel remote shared memory for MoE; abstract claims up to 10x faster expert switching than disk and CXL-like performance. Hardware and numbers beyond the abstract not read. Threatens novelty of "remote RAM as expert store" in the datacentre sense; the baseline is disk, so it does not speak to our laptop regime.
- exo and llama.cpp RDMA transport (from search digests of secondary blogs, not opened primary sources): exo supports Thunderbolt 5 RDMA with tensor/pipeline parallelism; a llama.cpp PR adds Apple RDMA as an RPC transport and claims 2.26x decode over TCP on a 2-node M3 Ultra test with a tiny Qwen3-0.6B model; Thunderbolt RDMA latency quoted as under 50 us vs 300 us. Unverified. These use the peer's compute (sharding), not a RAM-only store, so they are a different variant.
- llama.cpp RPC README: states RDMA instead of TCP is possible on Linux with RoCEv2 NICs (search digest only).
- CXL-NDP MoE (arXiv 2512.04476) and Beluga: datacentre far-memory, not our regime.

Memory check (from memory): "disaggregate experts onto remote memory" is well known in datacentres (MegaScale-Infer, DeepSeek's expert-parallel serving, Mooncake-style pooled memory, DeepSpeed-MoE). For consumer hardware, distributed-llama, Petals and exo are the closest projects; none treats a peer as a passive RAM store for batch-1 expert fetch that I know of. Our variant (one-sided reads from a peer's RAM, no peer compute) differs in a way that matters only if the peer link beats local NVMe; the MLX measurement says 3.7-5.2 GB/s on TB5, which is about local-NVMe speed.

Verdict #18: novelty known-to-variant (the architecture is known; the passive-store batch-1 consumer variant is only sketched publicly, in MLX issue 3208). Published evidence for a speedup in our regime: no (no source measures it; the only measured link in our regime, TB5, gives 3.7-5.2 GB/s, so the closed-form 1.49x serial at 8 GB falls to roughly 1.0-1.3x with those rates; this last figure is my arithmetic from F.md's own model, not measured). Our measurement: 1.49x serial / 1.00x overlap at 8 GB at 11 GB/s, about 1.0x at 16/32 GB. Headline numbers that cannot apply: MegaScale-Infer 1.90x (GPU, datacentre, large batch); issue 19745 15.9 tok/s (GPU, resident in GPU hosts); the Arm and RPC tok/s lines (not on the cited page).

## Family #19: quantized residual against the previous layer's predicted expert (idea P-0236)

Our measurement (F.md): real Qwen3-30B-A3B Q4_K_M weights, layers 10 vs 11, 192 pairs. Median |cos| 0.005, residual ratio 0.99999, bits saved 0.00001. Refuted as a bytes lever (about 1.0x). A planted correlation of 0.8 is detected, so the test has power. Limit: gate matrices only, 16 rows per expert, raw-neighbour base (no learned shared basis).

New prior art (searched 5 queries, opened 5):
- Meta Expert, "Cross-Layer Expert Sharing for Storage- and Retrieval-Efficient MoE", Hua et al., arXiv 2610.27598, 20 Oct 2026 (abstract only). Claims experts in different layers store similar content and that sharing matches a vanilla MoE with 70-80% of the experts. Model sizes not on the page, so unverified. Architecture-level (training time); it threatens "cross-layer similarity exists" but is not a post-hoc residual, and the claim sits against our 0.005 cosine measurement. Needs reading in full.
- ResMoE, Ai et al., arXiv 2503.06881 (KDD 2025): Wasserstein-barycenter shared expert plus residuals, up to 75% fewer expert parameters on Switch, Mixtral, DeepSeekMoE. Within layer, post-hoc. Strengthens the picture that any base is a within-layer, learned (not neighbouring-layer) object.
- MoBE, Chen et al., arXiv 2508.05257: per-expert A times layer-shared basis combination B; 24-30% fewer parameters at 1-2% accuracy drop on Qwen3-235B, DeepSeek-V3, Kimi-K2. Within layer. The most credible modest-compression published result at large scale; 24-30% only.
- DeltaMoE (ICLR 2025 workshop, search digest only): delta compression for merged SMoE, up to 2.34x compression; not opened.
- Search found no source comparing a layer's experts with the previous layer's experts element-wise. Novelty of the exact variant stays open, but our own test closes it empirically.

Memory check (from memory): shared base plus delta for experts is well known (MC-SMoE "Merge, then Compress", ICLR 2024; delta compression for fine-tuned LLMs such as BitDelta and Delta-CoMe). All of it is within-layer or between fine-tunes of one base, where weights share an ancestor. Our variant (base = a previous-layer expert chosen by a router predictor) has no ancestry argument, which is why the cosine is near zero. The router-predictor part (precision 0.55) is separate and known from expert-prefetch work.

Verdict #19: novelty: variant, and empirically dead (cross-layer weights are uncorrelated). Published evidence for a speedup in our regime: no. The within-layer methods get 24-75% parameter reduction with real quality cost (D2-MoE: PPL 23.6 vs 8.5 on Qwen3-30B at about 50%; MoBE 1-2% accuracy at 24-30%), and none measures load time from disk. Our measurement: bits saved 0.00001 per weight, bytes gain 1.00x. Caveat on our side: gate matrices only, no learned basis. Headline numbers that cannot apply: ResMoE 75%, DeRS 65% (upcycled models only), Trilemma 80% (small trained model), Meta Expert 70-80% of the experts (retraining).

- "Achieving Cloud-Grade SLOs for Local MoE Inference through CPU-GPU Hybrid Design", arXiv 2606.10493 (search digest only): reports CPU-side MoE prefill saturating beyond about 512 tokens and TTFT of about 20 s at 8K; relevant to the shape of our prefill-bound cost. Unopened.
- "Large Language Models Are Read/Write Policy-Makers for Simultaneous Generation", AAAI 2025 (arXiv 2501.00868): decides when to emit output while input is still arriving, for translation and ASR. The closest published relative of the early-emission part. It does not target prefill cost and does not verify against the full prompt. Variant threatens novelty a little. Search digest only.
- vLLM "resumable requests" for streaming input (Voxtral Realtime paper, arXiv 2602.11298, digest only): incremental prompt extension with KV reuse. Streaming input is known; emitting provisional answers and later reconciling is not found.

Memory check (from memory): chunked prefill (Sarathi, Orca-style splitting), prompt compression (LLMLingua, SnapKV-style pruning) and speculative prefill are well known. Simultaneous translation (wait-k, 2019) is the older home of "emit before the input ends". The combination of provisional tokens plus later reconciliation for prefill hiding is not in the literature I recall, but the idea's own statement is 0x on tok/s.

Verdict #20: novelty: variant of known pieces (chunked prefill plus streaming-input policies); the specific "provisional tokens, then reconcile" mix looks open, but its value is nil if the first block already costs 0.37-0.72 of the full prefill. Published evidence for a speedup in our regime: unclear to no (SpecPrefill/Cross-Family show 7-18x on GPU/RDU, where cost scales with tokens; layered prefill shows redundant expert re-reads are real, 12-39% on GPU). Our measurement: 1.4-2.7x earlier first block; prompt pruning 1.5-2.8x; the provisional-token quality question is unmeasured. Numbers that cannot apply: 7.66x, 46 s to 2.5 s, 70% TTFT cut, Sarathi capacity gains.

New prior art (searched 4 queries, opened 4):
- DraftExpert, Han, arXiv 2607.24434, 27 Jul 2026. Self-speculative decoding for expert-offloaded MoE on end devices (RTX 4090 with experts in host RAM; Snapdragon 8 Elite with experts staged from flash via llama.cpp Q4_0). Drafts use shared + top-1 expert + a small distilled draft expert; stops drafting when a token would add many new verifier experts. Average 1.45x decode speedup (DeepSeek-V2-Lite, Moonlight-16B-A3B), acceptance 84-87%. This is the most relevant new item: a published speculative scheme in an I/O-bound offloaded setting, 16B models only. It strengthens the premise that drafting must be expert-aware, and supports our finding that plain n-gram drafts cost expert unions. It needs training of a draft expert, unlike n-gram lookup.
- EcoSpec (Xie et al., arXiv 2607.12696, 14 Jul 2026): cost-aware draft-tree selection that penalises new experts; 8x H200, DeepSeek-V3.1, Qwen3-235B, GPT-OSS-120B; 1.14-1.36x average greedy over MTP/EAGLE-3 baselines. The intro's peak figures (1.50x, 1.47x) are not matched by its own greedy table (1.45x, 1.28x). GPU, resident experts. Same lesson, different regime.
- "The Limits of Speculation: Bounding Speculative Decoding in Mixture-of-Experts", Amankulov and Mamatin, arXiv 2609.22156 (Aug 2026): offline oracle for choosing speculation budget under MoE verification cost; Qwen3-Coder with EAGLE-3. Abstract only, no numbers. Supports the cost-aware framing.
- Lookahead decoding, Fu et al. 2024 (llama.cpp examples/lookahead, from the search digest and from memory) and REST (from memory): other n-gram/retrieval drafting schemes; prior art for the mechanism.
- MoESD (arXiv 2505.19645, listed in the MoE-Spec related work, not opened): when speculative decoding favours sparse MoEs.
- Search found no work on n-gram (prompt-lookup) drafting inside an SSD-offloaded CPU MoE.

Memory check (from memory): prompt lookup (Saxena 2023), llama.cpp lookup / lookahead examples, REST, Lookahead Decoding, Token Recycling and SAM Decoding all pre-date the screen and contain the mechanism; this family's core idea is thoroughly known. What is not covered is its interaction with a disk-bound expert cache, which MoE-Spec and DraftExpert address from the draft-tree side.

Verdict #21: novelty: known (the mechanism); the regime (tiny expert cache, disk-bound) is a variant that the MoE-specific papers (MoE-Spec, EcoSpec, DraftExpert) are moving toward. Published evidence for a speedup in our regime: unclear to no; n-gram speedups in the papers are dense or GPU-resident (SAM-Decoding 1.84x, PLD 2.4x on A100, Performance-or-Illusion up to roughly 1.5-2.1x on high-overlap workloads), and the one offloaded-MoE result (DraftExpert 1.45x) needs a trained draft expert. Our measurement: 0.74-1.03x on prose/code, 1.58-1.84x only on high-repetition text at 16/32 GB with the favourable batch model, and a loss at 8 GB. Numbers that cannot apply: 2.4x (A100, summarization), 2.58x (SAM+EAGLE-2, A6000), 1.90x-class GPU numbers, and EVICT's 32.5% figure (unverified).
